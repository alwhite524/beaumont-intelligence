Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation

Council Actions

Trace the Beaumont City Council decisions that authorized engineering, environmental work, project development, and the transition to RCTC-led delivery.

Foundation

2017–2018: environmental and engineering work begins

December 19, 2017

City records identify an original professional-services agreement with Moffatt & Nichol for environmental work associated with the Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation.

Later amendments show that the environmental effort continued for several years as the project moved through state and federal review requirements.

June 19, 2018

Beaumont entered into its engineering agreement with IDC Consulting Engineers.

Later City staff reports describe the initial engineering assignment as advancing Pennsylvania Avenue to approximately 35 percent design.

Early milestone

2019: approximately 35 percent design

Initial design completed

The City's July 2025 Pennsylvania Avenue status report states that approximately 35 percent design was completed in 2019.

The record then describes a period in which the project did not immediately advance to final design because additional funding was needed.

This is important context for later Council actions: the project did not proceed continuously from early engineering directly into construction.

Design restart

July 2022: IDC agreement amended

Second amendment

In July 2022, Council considered a second amendment to IDC's professional-services agreement for approximately $1.295 million in additional engineering scope.

The action was intended to advance the Pennsylvania Avenue project toward final design.

Later limitation identified

The City's July 2025 status report later stated that the 2022 amendment did not include all scope required to complete the project.

That later finding is significant because it helps explain why additional design authorizations followed in 2024.

Environmental work

November 2022: additional environmental authorization

Moffatt & Nichol amendment

Beaumont continued amending the environmental-services agreement as additional documentation and technical studies were required.

A November 2022 Council item addressed another amendment for environmental work associated with the grade-separation project.

Later records show that environmental processing remained an active project issue through 2025.

Expanded final design

October 1, 2024: agreements restructured

1

IDC amended and restated agreement

Council considered an amended and restated professional-services agreement with IDC with authorization not to exceed approximately $1.884 million.

2

Environmental agreement

Council also considered revised environmental consulting authorization associated with completing project-development requirements.

3

Completing the missing scope

City records explain that the updated agreements were intended to provide the remaining scope necessary to complete the project and position Pennsylvania Avenue for outside funding.

4

Design remained in progress

The 2024 staff materials identified major design components at different completion levels, including railroad structural work, track design, Caltrans-related plans, and civil engineering.

Funding milestone

2025: the state funding opportunity changes the stakes

By 2025, Pennsylvania Avenue was no longer only a long-term City capital project.

The project had secured access to a major state construction-funding opportunity through the Trade Corridor Enhancement Program.

That funding came with schedule and allocation requirements, increasing the importance of environmental clearance, final design, right-of-way, utility coordination, and construction readiness.

The widely reported $50.4 million TCEP package consists of $49.4 million for Pennsylvania Avenue grade-separation construction and $1 million for a separate Beaumont Park & Ride EV-charging component.
Key Council record

July 15, 2025: Pennsylvania status update

Council receives the project history

Council received a dedicated Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation Update for CIP 2017-012.

The report reconstructed several important project milestones:

  • IDC's engineering agreement began in 2018.
  • Approximately 35 percent design was completed in 2019.
  • Project development slowed while additional funding was pursued.
  • The 2022 IDC amendment did not contain all scope necessary to complete final design.
  • The 2024 agreement was intended to provide the remaining design scope and improve the project's ability to compete for outside funding.

The Council action was to receive and file the update.

Environmental decision

November 18, 2025: project and environmental approvals

Environmental record completed

Council considered the project's completed environmental documentation after an extended environmental-review process involving Beaumont and Caltrans.

State records later attributed nearly 11 months of project delay to changes in the environmental review approach.

Project approval

Council approved the Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation project and the associated environmental actions needed to advance the project into its next delivery phase.

Major delivery decision

November 18, 2025: Beaumont brings in RCTC

Cooperative agreement — up to $14 million

Council approved a cooperative agreement with the Riverside County Transportation Commission for Pennsylvania Avenue PS&E and right-of-way project delivery.

The agreement authorized City-supported funding in an amount not to exceed $14 million.

RCTC records subsequently characterized these funds as local-fund contribution revenue supporting Pennsylvania Avenue project expenses.

Important: The $14 million agreement should not be described as a $14 million grant from RCTC to Beaumont. It is the authorized funding framework for Beaumont-supported RCTC project-delivery work.
Why the transition mattered

The Council action changed how Pennsylvania would be delivered

1

City-led development

Beaumont had directly managed the environmental and engineering effort through multiple consultants for several years.

2

Funding deadline pressure

The major state construction award created a time-sensitive need to complete environmental, design, right-of-way, utility, and railroad work.

3

RCTC coordination

Beaumont requested RCTC assistance because of critical funding timing requirements and the complexity of delivering a multi-agency railroad grade separation.

4

Consolidated delivery

RCTC moved toward consolidating major design, railroad, environmental-support, and right-of-way functions under a more integrated delivery team.

After Council authorization

RCTC actions became increasingly important

Once Beaumont authorized the cooperative agreement, major delivery decisions increasingly appeared in Riverside County Transportation Commission records rather than only in Beaumont City Council agendas.

HDR PS&E agreement

RCTC approved a professional-services agreement with HDR for approximately $5.874 million, plus approximately $587,000 contingency.

The maximum authorization was approximately $6.462 million.

Existing project knowledge

HDR was already participating in Pennsylvania Avenue railroad and track-design work before being selected for the consolidated PS&E role.

Implementing agency changes

The California Transportation Commission later changed the implementing agency for the grade-separation component from Beaumont to RCTC.

Funding extension

RCTC pursued additional time to protect the $49.4 million state construction allocation while project-development work continued.

Council accountability

What did Beaumont retain after RCTC took the lead?

Transferring major project-delivery responsibilities to RCTC did not eliminate Beaumont's role in Pennsylvania Avenue.

Project sponsorship

Pennsylvania Avenue remains a Beaumont infrastructure priority and a project initiated through the City's capital-improvement program.

Local funding

Beaumont authorized substantial local financial support for RCTC-led final design and right-of-way delivery.

Construction funding gap

RCTC records indicate that Beaumont remains responsible for addressing project funding needs not covered by existing state and other funding sources.

Future agreements

Construction and construction-management delivery will require later agreements, amendments, authorizations, or related public actions.

How to interpret Council records

Five different kinds of evidence

1

Staff Recommendation

What City staff asked Council to approve. A recommendation is not itself a final Council action.

2

Council Action

The action actually taken by the elected body, ideally verified through minutes or video.

3

Contract Authorization

Authority to enter into an agreement up to a stated amount. It does not prove the full amount was ultimately spent.

4

Executed Agreement

The signed legal agreement establishing responsibilities among the participating parties.

5

Implementation

What happened after authorization: design, payment, construction, amendment, delay, or later project modification.

Intelligence assessment

Council decisions tell only part of the Pennsylvania story

For much of Pennsylvania Avenue's history, Beaumont City Council records provide the primary public trail of consultant agreements, project development, environmental work, and funding decisions.

That changed materially in late 2025.

Once Council authorized the cooperative agreement with RCTC, many of the most consequential project-delivery decisions began appearing in RCTC and California Transportation Commission records.

Understanding Pennsylvania Avenue therefore requires following several public bodies rather than looking only at Beaumont Council agendas.

The November 2025 Council action did not end Beaumont's responsibility for the project. It changed the structure through which the project would be delivered.
