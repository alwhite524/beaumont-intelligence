Source 1

City of Beaumont Project Record

Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation Project Page

Beaumont's official project page provides the City's description of the grade separation and identifies the major transportation and safety improvements.

The project replaces the existing Union Pacific Railroad at-grade crossing with an underpass while preserving four roadway lanes and adding pedestrian, ramp, and traffic-signal improvements.

The page also preserves an earlier City project-cost figure of approximately $78 million.

Source 2

Beaumont's 2026 Project Update

Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation Receives Federal Funding

Beaumont's June 2026 announcement is the clearest current City statement concerning project cost and near-term schedule.

The City reports an approximately $140 million project estimate, identifies an additional $2 million federal funding contribution, and states that the project is targeting Ready-to-List status in June 2027.

This source is particularly important because the approximately $140 million figure is substantially higher than earlier published project estimates.

Source 3

California Environmental Record

CEQAnet — Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation

The state environmental record provides one of the most detailed official descriptions of the physical project.

It documents the depressed Pennsylvania Avenue roadway, approximately 105-foot railroad bridge, I-10 ramp modifications, new traffic signals, retaining walls, ADA improvements, sewer relocation, and temporary railroad shoofly.

The record also confirms Caltrans' role because portions of the project affect the state highway system.

Source 4

State Baseline Agreement

2026 Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation Baseline

The California Transportation Commission baseline is one of the most important financial and project-delivery records in the archive.

It establishes the project's state-programmed scope, phases, implementing agencies, schedule, and cost assumptions used for state accountability.

The baseline identifies RCTC as the implementing agency for major remaining phases and documents a programmed project cost of approximately $78.9 million within the baseline structure.

That figure should not be treated as identical to Beaumont's later approximately $140 million whole-project estimate. The two records were prepared for different purposes and at different stages.

Source 5

Implementing Agency Transfer

California Transportation Commission — March 2026

This California Transportation Commission record formally documents one of the central turning points in the project.

Caltrans and Beaumont requested that the implementing agency for the Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation component be changed from the City of Beaumont to the Riverside County Transportation Commission.

The state record specifically cites RCTC's experience delivering complex, multi-agency grade-separation projects as support for the change.

It also identifies $49.4 million programmed for the construction phase.

State Funding Deadline Record

2026 Construction Allocation Extension

The California Transportation Commission allocation-extension record is critical for understanding why project schedule became a funding issue.

The record connects environmental completion, the transition to RCTC, final project development, and the deadline for preserving state construction funding.

This source helps distinguish between a project simply being delayed and a project facing specific funding-program deadlines that had to be managed.

Source 7

Beaumont Council Project History

July 15, 2025 Project Status Update

This Council record is one of the strongest sources for reconstructing how the project reached its later delivery problems.

The staff history traces engineering work beginning in 2018, development to approximately 35-percent design in 2019, a funding-related pause, later restart of design work, and the limits of the subsequent consultant scope.

It provides essential context for understanding why reaching final design required additional work years after engineering had originally begun.

Source 8

Engineering Scope Record

IDC Detailed Scope and Fee Proposal

The detailed engineering scope is important because it shows how many specialized disciplines became involved in completing the project.

The scope documents coordination involving roadway engineering, railroad work, right-of-way, utilities, traffic, Caltrans facilities, and other technical disciplines.

It helps explain why Pennsylvania Avenue should not be understood as a simple roadway underpass project.

Source 9

Beaumont–RCTC Delivery Transition

November 18, 2025 Council Record

This meeting represents another major turning point.

The Council considered environmental approval and the cooperative framework that shifted significant remaining project-delivery responsibilities, including final design and right-of-way work, toward RCTC.

Read together with later RCTC and CTC records, this meeting helps establish that the change in project leadership was a formal, multi-agency delivery decision.

Source 10

RCTC Delivery Record

RCTC Western Riverside County Programs and Projects

RCTC's records document the agency's assumption of a larger project-delivery role.

These materials address the Beaumont cooperative agreement, final-design responsibilities, consultant organization, funding responsibilities, and the work required to move the project toward bidding.

RCTC records are essential because the later stages of Pennsylvania Avenue can no longer be reconstructed from Beaumont Council records alone.

Historical benchmark

Earlier Cost Record

2023 Beaumont Local Hazard Mitigation Plan

Beaumont's 2023 mitigation plan provides a valuable dated benchmark in the project's cost history.

At that time, the City described the Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation as an approximately $45 million project and identified it as unfunded.

The record is particularly useful when compared with the later approximately $78 million state baseline and approximately $140 million current City estimate.

Read the record together

What the primary sources establish

1

The project changed

The final project involves substantially more than simply lowering Pennsylvania Avenue beneath the railroad.

2

The cost changed

Official records preserve materially different cost estimates as the project advanced and its scope became better defined.

3

The delivery structure changed

Responsibility evolved from a primarily City-managed project toward RCTC-led delivery for major remaining phases.

4

The schedule matters financially

State construction funding is tied to formal programming and allocation deadlines.

5

Multiple agencies control the outcome

Beaumont, RCTC, Caltrans, the California Transportation Commission, Union Pacific, and other entities all affect project delivery.

Cost history

Do not collapse the estimates into one number

2023 benchmark

Approximately $45 million

Beaumont Local Hazard Mitigation Plan.

State baseline

Approximately $78.9 million

California Transportation Commission baseline-programming record.

Current City estimate

Approximately $140 million

Beaumont's June 2026 public project update.

These figures should be presented as dated official estimates, not as interchangeable measures. Differences may reflect project maturity, expanded or better-defined scope, agency requirements, railroad requirements, right-of-way, utilities, escalation, construction support, contingencies, or differences in what each estimate includes. Beaumont Intelligence should not attribute the increase to any single cause unless an official record expressly establishes that cause.
Evidence discipline

What the record does not yet prove

One definitive cause of the cost increase

The evidence identifies numerous sources of project complexity, but we have not located a single official reconciliation explaining every dollar between historical estimates and the current approximately $140 million figure.

Final construction price

The project has not yet produced a construction bid and award establishing the final market price.

Final completion date

Ready-to-List is a project-development milestone, not a construction-completion date.

Final local financial exposure

The eventual City and regional contribution will depend on final cost, available grants, programmed funding, bids, and future project actions.

Research conclusion

A multi-agency project leaves a multi-agency record

No single document tells the Pennsylvania Avenue story.

Beaumont's records explain the project's origins, early engineering work, consultant decisions, and local approvals.

Environmental records define the physical project that ultimately had to be delivered.

RCTC records explain the transition toward a consolidated regional delivery structure.

California Transportation Commission records establish the state funding framework, deadlines, baseline, and implementing-agency change.

Read together, those records show a project that became more technically complicated, more expensive, and more institutionally complex as it moved from concept toward construction.
