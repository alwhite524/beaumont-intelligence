Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation

Project Design

See how Pennsylvania Avenue, the Union Pacific Railroad, Interstate 10, utilities, drainage, and surrounding properties must be reconstructed as one coordinated project.

More than a railroad bridge The project must separate road and rail traffic while maintaining railroad operations, freeway access, utilities, and local circulation during construction.

The basic idea

Pennsylvania Avenue goes under the railroad

Today, Pennsylvania Avenue crosses the Union Pacific Railroad at street level. Vehicles must cross the active railroad tracks, creating an at-grade crossing between road and rail traffic.

The grade-separation project removes that conflict.

Pennsylvania Avenue will be lowered beneath the railroad while the railroad is carried across the roadway on a new bridge. The result is a permanent physical separation between vehicle traffic and train operations.

Plain English: Pennsylvania Avenue is not being carried over the railroad. The roadway is being reconstructed below it, while the railroad remains at approximately its existing elevation on a new structure.
Design at a glance

Road below. Railroad above.

The project separates Pennsylvania Avenue from the railroad by lowering the roadway beneath a new railroad bridge. Trains remain on the rail corridor above while vehicles, bicycles, and pedestrians pass underneath.

Design at a glance

What has to be built?

1

Lower Pennsylvania Avenue

The roadway must descend from the surrounding street elevation to pass beneath the railroad and then climb back toward the Interstate 10 interchange.

2

Build a railroad bridge

A new railroad structure will carry Union Pacific operations over the reconstructed Pennsylvania Avenue.

3

Maintain railroad operations

Active freight operations cannot simply stop while the permanent bridge is being constructed.

4

Reconstruct the roadway

Pennsylvania Avenue will be rebuilt as a wider multimodal roadway through the project limits.

5

Rework I-10 connections

The roadway profile and intersection system must continue functioning with the nearby Interstate 10 ramps and Caltrans facilities.

6

Move utilities

Existing underground and overhead infrastructure conflicts with the lowered roadway and other construction elements.

Roadway

Reconstructing Pennsylvania Avenue

Grade separation changes the vertical profile of the roadway. Pennsylvania Avenue must descend sufficiently to provide clearance beneath the railroad structure and then transition back into the existing street and freeway-interchange system.

Four roadway lanes

Project records describe a four-lane Pennsylvania Avenue through the reconstructed corridor.

Retaining structures

Lowering the roadway creates substantial elevation differences between Pennsylvania Avenue and adjacent property, requiring retaining walls and related structures.

Pedestrian facilities

The reconstructed corridor includes pedestrian improvements so people can move through the grade-separated crossing without using the railroad tracks.

Bicycle accommodation

The corridor design must also account for multimodal transportation needs as Pennsylvania Avenue is rebuilt.

Railroad

Building beneath an operating freight corridor

The Union Pacific Railroad is not simply an obstacle crossed by the project. It is an operating transportation system that must remain functional while construction occurs.

That requirement substantially affects engineering, construction sequencing, cost, and schedule.

New railroad bridge

The permanent project includes a railroad bridge carrying the tracks over Pennsylvania Avenue.

Track design

Railroad track geometry must be coordinated with the bridge, approaches, existing rail corridor, and Union Pacific operating requirements.

Railroad approvals

Union Pacific review is required for work affecting its tracks, structures, right-of-way, operations, and construction sequencing.

Railroad protection

Construction near active tracks requires specialized railroad safety procedures, coordination, and protective measures.

Construction sequencing

Why a temporary railroad track may be necessary

One of the central construction problems is straightforward: the new railroad bridge must be built where active railroad operations already exist.

The project therefore requires a construction strategy that allows trains to continue operating while the permanent grade separation is built.

Temporary shoofly

Project records describe temporary railroad alignment work commonly referred to as a shoofly.

A shoofly temporarily shifts railroad operations away from the permanent track alignment so construction can occur in the existing railroad corridor.

After the permanent railroad bridge and track improvements are ready, rail operations can be shifted onto the completed alignment and the temporary facilities removed.

Why this matters: Maintaining an active railroad while building a bridge beneath it requires temporary construction that would not exist if the railroad could simply be closed. That temporary work affects project footprint, engineering, right-of-way, schedule, and cost.
Future rail capacity

Designing for more than today's tracks

The railroad structure must account not only for existing Union Pacific operations but also for future rail capacity contemplated within the corridor.

Future third track

Project records identify accommodation for a future third railroad track.

That requirement influences the width and configuration of the railroad structure even though the additional track may not be placed into service as part of the initial Pennsylvania Avenue construction.

Important distinction: Designing the structure to accommodate a future track does not necessarily mean the Pennsylvania Avenue project itself will construct and activate that future rail service.
Interstate 10

The freeway makes the project more complicated

Pennsylvania Avenue reaches Interstate 10 almost immediately south of the railroad crossing.

That means the grade separation cannot be designed independently from the freeway interchange.

I-10 ramps

The roadway must connect appropriately with the existing Interstate 10 ramp system while Pennsylvania Avenue's vertical profile is being changed.

Caltrans review

Work affecting the freeway interchange requires coordination and approval through Caltrans.

Traffic operations

Signal timing, turning movements, queues, freeway access, and local circulation must function with the redesigned corridor.

Design standards

The project must satisfy applicable Caltrans requirements where City roadway improvements interact with the state highway system.

The design constraint: The railroad establishes one major elevation requirement while the I-10 interchange establishes another. Pennsylvania Avenue must transition between both systems within a relatively short distance.
Utilities

Infrastructure already occupies the corridor

Lowering Pennsylvania Avenue affects infrastructure that currently runs within or across the roadway corridor.

Those facilities cannot simply remain in place where they conflict with excavation, retaining walls, foundations, drainage, or the new roadway profile.

Sewer

Sewer infrastructure requires relocation and specialized construction where project geometry and Interstate 10 create significant constraints.

Water

Water infrastructure within the project area must be coordinated with the reconstructed roadway and structural work.

Dry utilities

Electrical, communications, gas, and other utility facilities may require protection, relocation, or redesign.

Utility schedules

Utility work can affect when roadway and structural construction can proceed, making coordination part of the project's critical delivery sequence.

Drainage

Lowering a roadway changes how water behaves

An undercrossing creates a low point in the roadway. Unlike a normal surface street, stormwater cannot always continue draining downhill through conventional gravity systems.

Low-point drainage

The roadway must be designed so stormwater does not collect within the depressed Pennsylvania Avenue section.

Stormwater infrastructure

Drainage facilities must be coordinated with retaining structures, roadway grades, utilities, and surrounding drainage systems.

Drainage is therefore part of the permanent grade-separation infrastructure rather than a minor roadway detail.
Right-of-way

The project extends beyond today's pavement

The physical footprint required for the project is larger than the existing Pennsylvania Avenue roadway.

Permanent property needs

Wider roadway sections, retaining structures, utilities, railroad facilities, and other improvements can require additional permanent property interests.

Temporary construction areas

Construction staging, access, temporary railroad work, utility relocation, and grading can require property beyond the final permanent project footprint.

Easements

Utility, drainage, access, and other infrastructure may require permanent or temporary easements.

Acquisition schedule

Required property interests must be secured before affected portions of construction can proceed.

Agencies

No single agency controls the entire project

1

City of Beaumont

Project sponsor and owner of the local roadway system, with a long history of planning and advancing the Pennsylvania Avenue improvement.

2

RCTC

Riverside County Transportation Commission now leads major elements of final project delivery, including PS&E and right-of-way coordination.

3

Union Pacific

Controls the active railroad corridor and must review and approve work affecting railroad infrastructure and operations.

4

Caltrans

Controls the Interstate 10 state highway facilities that directly interact with the Pennsylvania Avenue corridor.

5

California Transportation Commission

Oversees the state TCEP funding commitment, baseline agreement, allocation milestones, and related programming requirements.

6

Utility agencies

Public and private utility owners must coordinate relocation and protection of infrastructure affected by the project.

The design challenge

Several projects are effectively happening at once

1

A roadway project

Pennsylvania Avenue is widened, lowered, reconstructed, and reconnected to the surrounding street system.

2

A railroad project

Active tracks, temporary railroad operations, a permanent bridge, and future rail capacity must all be accommodated.

3

A freeway-interface project

Pennsylvania Avenue must continue connecting with Interstate 10 under Caltrans requirements.

4

A utility project

Existing infrastructure must be moved, protected, or reconstructed around the new roadway and structures.

5

A drainage project

The depressed roadway requires a permanent strategy for safely handling stormwater.

6

A property project

Right-of-way and temporary construction needs must be resolved before portions of the work can move forward.

Why the design evolved

Early concepts were not construction-ready plans

Pennsylvania Avenue has been studied for years, but an early project concept is different from a construction-ready design.

As engineering advanced, the project had to incorporate increasingly detailed requirements from Union Pacific, Caltrans, utility owners, environmental review, right-of-way needs, and construction sequencing.

Those requirements affected the physical project, not merely the paperwork surrounding it.

Key context: The growth in project cost and schedule cannot be understood only by comparing an early estimate with a later estimate. The project being priced later in development contains requirements, risks, temporary construction, agency conditions, and delivery details that were not necessarily defined to the same degree when earlier estimates were prepared.
What residents ultimately get

The purpose of the completed project

No at-grade railroad crossing

Vehicles and pedestrians will no longer need to cross active railroad tracks at street level.

Improved emergency access

Emergency vehicles will not be blocked at Pennsylvania Avenue by trains occupying the crossing.

More reliable circulation

Local traffic will be physically separated from railroad operations at the crossing.

Safer rail-road interface

The permanent grade separation removes the direct conflict between roadway users and trains.

Rebuilt corridor

Pennsylvania Avenue will emerge as a substantially reconstructed transportation corridor rather than simply an existing street with a bridge added to it.

Long-term infrastructure

The project is intended to accommodate future transportation needs, including additional railroad capacity contemplated in the corridor.

Intelligence assessment

Why Pennsylvania Avenue became so complicated

Pennsylvania Avenue is sometimes described simply as a railroad grade-separation project.

Technically, that is correct. Practically, it understates the project.

The design must fit a lowered City roadway beneath an operating Union Pacific freight corridor while reconnecting that roadway almost immediately to an Interstate 10 interchange controlled by Caltrans.

At the same time, engineers must maintain railroad operations, relocate utilities, provide drainage for a depressed roadway, acquire necessary property interests, accommodate future rail capacity, and develop a construction sequence that allows transportation systems around the site to continue functioning.

That combination helps explain why Pennsylvania Avenue developed from a relatively straightforward early concept into one of Beaumont's most technically complicated transportation projects.
