Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation

Project Delivery

How Pennsylvania Avenue moved from City-managed project development to RCTC-led final design and right-of-way delivery—and what that transition means.

A change in delivery—not ownership RCTC assuming the lead did not erase Beaumont's financial responsibilities or its interest in the project.

The central question

Why did RCTC take the lead?

Pennsylvania Avenue began as a City of Beaumont capital project. For years, Beaumont directly retained the environmental, engineering, railroad, utility, and other consultants needed to advance the project.

By late 2025, however, the project had reached a critical point.

Beaumont had secured access to a major state construction funding award, but environmental clearance, final design, right-of-way, utility work, railroad coordination, and other delivery requirements remained incomplete.

RCTC's own explanation: Beaumont requested RCTC assistance because of critical funding timing requirements associated with the project. The transition was therefore closely tied to protecting the project's ability to use the state construction funding.
Before RCTC

A City-led, multi-consultant delivery structure

Beaumont's project-development model involved several specialized firms working on different parts of Pennsylvania Avenue.

1

IDC Consulting Engineers

IDC served as the City's principal engineering consultant and advanced the project from early design toward Plans, Specifications & Estimates.

2

Moffatt & Nichol

Moffatt & Nichol performed environmental and railroad-related work over multiple years.

3

HDR

HDR was already part of the Pennsylvania design team, including railroad and track-related work, before RCTC later selected the firm for the consolidated final-design role.

4

Specialty consultants

Additional firms supported right-of-way, signals, traffic analysis, utilities, surveying, geotechnical work, railroad coordination, and other disciplines.

The delivery problem

Several contracts had to function as one project

The individual consultant disciplines were all necessary. The challenge was coordinating them as the project approached a time-sensitive construction-funding deadline.

RCTC's assessment

RCTC described the project as being developed through several separate, non-linked consultant contracts.

Its staff recommended consolidating major project-delivery functions to reduce coordination and design conflicts, improve accountability, and increase the likelihood of meeting critical funding milestones.

What this does not prove: The record does not establish that the earlier design work was useless or entirely duplicated. In fact, the later RCTC team incorporated firms and technical knowledge already involved in Pennsylvania Avenue.
November 2025

Beaumont authorizes the RCTC partnership

Cooperative agreement

On November 18, 2025, Beaumont City Council approved a cooperative agreement with the Riverside County Transportation Commission.

PS&E and Right-of-Way

The agreement authorized RCTC to lead major final-design and right-of-way delivery activities for Pennsylvania Avenue.

Up to $14 million

Beaumont authorized funding not to exceed $14 million for the RCTC-led project-delivery effort.

Local contribution

RCTC accounting records characterize the $14 million as local-fund contribution revenue supporting Pennsylvania Avenue project expenses.

Important distinction: The $14 million is not a grant from RCTC to Beaumont. It is an authorized funding framework for Beaumont-supported project-delivery work performed through RCTC.
Why RCTC?

Experience with complicated transportation delivery

Pennsylvania Avenue requires coordination among a City roadway, an active Union Pacific freight corridor, Interstate 10 facilities controlled by Caltrans, utility companies, property owners, state funding agencies, and specialized engineering teams.

1

Railroad experience

RCTC has experience advancing grade-separation projects involving active freight railroads and complex railroad agreements.

2

State-agency coordination

RCTC regularly works with Caltrans and the California Transportation Commission on major transportation funding and delivery requirements.

3

Right-of-Way

RCTC has institutional experience acquiring property interests and managing right-of-way for regionally significant transportation projects.

4

Construction preparation

RCTC can coordinate design, procurement, construction management, utility relocation, and other activities across a larger transportation-delivery organization.

When RCTC selected HDR for the consolidated Plans, Specifications & Estimates effort, HDR was not entering Pennsylvania Avenue for the first time.

The firm had already participated in railroad and track-design elements of the project.

What RCTC inherited

A partially developed project—not a blank sheet of paper

Completed early design

Approximately 35 percent design had been completed years earlier.

Advanced engineering

Railroad structures, civil plans, track work, and Caltrans-related design had advanced beyond the earliest concept stage.

Environmental work

Years of environmental studies and coordination had already occurred before RCTC assumed the lead.

Unfinished delivery work

Final design, right-of-way, utilities, railroad coordination, and other tasks still required substantial work.

The RCTC transition is therefore best understood as a change in how the remaining project would be coordinated and delivered.

State-level change

RCTC becomes the implementing agency

The transition eventually became more than a local cooperative arrangement.

In 2026, the California Transportation Commission formally changed the implementing agency for the Pennsylvania Avenue grade-separation component from the City of Beaumont to RCTC.

Why that matters

The implementing agency is the organization responsible within the state funding structure for advancing the funded project component and meeting applicable delivery requirements.

The formal change aligned the state funding structure with the RCTC-led delivery arrangement already underway.

Funding pressure

The $49.4 million deadline influenced project delivery

The Pennsylvania Avenue construction component received $49.4 million through the state's Trade Corridor Enhancement Program.

That funding was tied to project-delivery milestones.

Original deadline

The original construction-allocation deadline was June 30, 2026.

Project was not ready

Environmental clearance, right-of-way, utilities, railroad coordination, and final design had not advanced sufficiently to meet the original delivery schedule.

Funding risk

RCTC public discussion acknowledged that failure to meet the applicable state milestones could place the construction funding at risk.

Extension

RCTC pursued additional time, moving the construction-allocation target toward June 2027.

Responsibilities

What moved to RCTC—and what did not?

RCTC-led responsibilities

  • Final design / PS&E coordination
  • Right-of-way delivery
  • Consultant coordination
  • Major railroad-delivery coordination
  • State funding and project-delivery coordination
  • Preparation for later construction delivery

Beaumont responsibilities remain

  • Local project sponsorship and policy decisions
  • Local funding obligations under the cooperative agreement
  • Addressing funding not covered by existing grants
  • Future City approvals or agreements where required
  • Coordination involving City streets, utilities, and local interests
  • Long-term ownership and operation of applicable City infrastructure
Other agencies

RCTC still cannot act alone

1

Union Pacific Railroad

Controls the active railroad corridor and must approve work affecting railroad infrastructure and operations.

2

Caltrans

Controls the Interstate 10 facilities that interact with Pennsylvania Avenue and must approve applicable freeway-related design.

3

California Transportation Commission

Controls major state funding actions, project programming, baseline requirements, and allocation deadlines.

4

Utility owners

Control infrastructure that must be relocated, protected, or modified before and during construction.

5

Property owners

Right-of-way acquisition and temporary construction needs may affect private or public property interests.

6

Federal funding agencies

Federal dollars bring their own eligibility, documentation, and project-delivery requirements.

Financial responsibility

RCTC taking the lead did not mean RCTC took the funding gap

This is one of the most important distinctions in the Pennsylvania Avenue record.

Project delivery versus project funding

RCTC can lead the technical and administrative delivery of Pennsylvania Avenue without becoming financially responsible for every project cost.

RCTC discussion made clear that the state construction grant was not expected to cover the entire project and that Beaumont remained responsible for addressing the funding gap.

RCTC also indicated that it was not assuming an obligation to make up that shortfall.

Plain English: RCTC took responsibility for helping get the project delivered. It did not simply take over Beaumont's entire financial obligation.
Construction

The current agreement does not finish the delivery story

Beaumont's current cooperative agreement with RCTC centers on final design and right-of-way delivery.

Construction is a later phase.

Construction contract

A construction contract must eventually be advertised, bid, funded, and awarded.

Construction management

A separate team will be needed to administer, inspect, coordinate, and document construction.

Future agreements

RCTC records indicate that future construction and construction-management responsibilities will require additional agreements or amendments.

Future funding decisions

Additional public actions may be required as final construction costs and remaining funding needs become clearer.

Before and after

How project delivery changed

Earlier model

  • Beaumont served as the primary delivery agency.
  • Multiple specialized consultant contracts.
  • City directly managed project coordination.
  • Funding pursued while design evolved.
  • Consultant scopes expanded as requirements developed.

Current model

  • RCTC leads PS&E and right-of-way delivery.
  • HDR serves as consolidated final-design consultant.
  • RCTC coordinates major delivery functions.
  • CTC recognizes RCTC as implementing agency for the grade-separation component.
  • Beaumont continues providing local financial and policy support.
Intelligence assessment

The transition was a project-management decision driven by delivery risk

The available record does not support describing RCTC's involvement as a simple takeover of a failed City project.

Beaumont had already advanced years of environmental, roadway, railroad, and engineering work and had secured a major state construction-funding opportunity.

At the same time, the project had become more complicated than the earlier consultant structure was well positioned to deliver within the state's funding timeline.

Environmental clearance had taken longer than anticipated. Right-of-way and utility work remained. Union Pacific coordination was a major long-lead requirement. Final design still required substantial effort. And the construction funding carried a deadline.

RCTC's role was therefore designed to consolidate responsibility, bring regional grade-separation expertise to the project, and improve the likelihood that Pennsylvania Avenue could reach construction without losing critical funding.

But the change in delivery structure does not eliminate Beaumont's continuing financial exposure or future policy decisions.
