 Staff Report 
 
 
TO:  Honorable Mayor and Members of the City Council 
FROM: Robert L. Vestal, Director of Public Works | City Engineer 
DATE: August 4, 2026 
SUBJECT:  Pennsylvania Grade Separation – Funding Strategy 
  
Description:  Discussion regarding the funding strategy for the Pennsylvania Avenue 
Grade Separation Project, including the Trade Corridor Enhancement Program (TCEP), 
additional grant opportunities, current project progress, and ongoing efforts to advance 
the City’s number one Strategic Plan priority toward construction. 
 
Motion: 
If the City Council determines that staff’s recommendation is appropriate, the following 
motion is provided: 
 
I move to authorize the City Manager to withdraw the Pennsylvania Grade Separation 
Project from the 2024 Trade Corridor Enhancement Program (TCEP), and to reapply for 
the 2026 TCEP cycle.      
 
Recommendation: 
The recommended withdrawal is a strategic funding decision and does not represent a 
change in the City’s commitment to the Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation Project. 
The project remains the City’s number one Strategic Plan priority, and RCTC and City 
staff will continue advancing design, right-of-way, environmental permitting, utility 
coordination, and required agreements with Caltrans and Union Pacific Railroad 
(UPRR) based on the current project schedule. At the same time, staff will continue 
aggressively pursuing federal, state, regional, and other funding opportunities 
necessary to close the remaining construction funding gap and position the project for 
delivery at the earliest feasible date. 
 
Discussion:  
On July 21, 2026, the Riverside County Transportation Commission (RCTC) notified the 
City of concerns regarding the Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation Project’s ability 
to meet the funding deadlines established under the 2024 TCEP. Under the current

program requirements, construction funds must be allocated by June 2027, which 
already reflects a previously approved one-year extension. 
To meet the allocation deadline, the Project must achieve Ready to List (RTL) status. 
RTL requires completion or sufficient resolution of environmental compliance, right-of-
way certification, final plans, specifications and estimates, environmental and 
construction permits, utility coordination, funding and programming requirements, 
constructability and risk reviews, and required agreements with Caltrans and UPRR, 
including the UPRR Construction and Maintenance Agreement. 
RCTC identified several schedule risks that could affect the Project’s ability to complete 
these requirements by June 2027. These include third-party agency review and 
approval timelines, utility relocation coordination, environmental permitting, right-of-way 
acquisition, coordination with the Master Drainage Plan Line 2 Project, required 
agreements with UPRR and Caltrans, and the remaining project funding gap. Staff has 
reviewed the concerns identified by RCTC and agrees that, despite continued progress, 
several critical schedule elements are dependent upon outside agencies and processes 
that the City and RCTC do not fully control. 
If the Project receives a TCEP award but is unable to achieve RTL by the allocation 
deadline, per adopted guidelines, the California Transportation Commission will 
deprogram the funding. Such an outcome could negatively affect the City’s standing in 
future competitive funding cycles and potentially result in greater scrutiny of project 
readiness. 
For this reason, staff recommends voluntarily withdrawing from the current TCEP cycle 
and reapplying in the 2026 cycle. This approach provides additional time to advance the 
Project, resolve remaining third-party requirements, and submit a stronger application 
based on a substantially more mature project. It may also provide an opportunity to 
request additional TCEP funding to address the Project’s current funding gap. 
Reapplication is not without risk. TCEP is highly competitive, and there is no guarantee 
that the Project will receive an award in a future cycle. Construction costs may also 
continue to increase, and the City will be required to prepare an updated application, 
benefit calculations, and supporting materials. Both City and RCTC staff believes, 
however, that these risks are preferable to proceeding with the current award that 
includes a schedule that has a significant risk of not meeting the required allocation 
deadline. 
Importantly, withdrawal from the 2024 TCEP cycle is a funding strategy decision, not a 
decision to delay or suspend the Project. Staff intends to continue advancing the Project 
according to the existing schedule and will proceed with the work necessary to position 
the Project for construction as soon as funding and required approvals are secured.

Additional Grant Opportunities 
The City is actively pursuing additional federal funding to close the remaining 
construction funding gap. The City recently submitted a Railroad Crossing Elimination 
Program application requesting approximately $45.5 million. The requested federal 
share represents approximately one-third of the Project cost, with the non-federal 
contribution substantially exceeding the program’s minimum match requirement. 
Results are anticipated in January 2027. 
The City also submitted a Consolidated Rail Infrastructure and Safety Improvements 
grant application requesting approximately $54.8 million. Based on the current 
estimated Project cost of approximately $147.6 million, the Project includes 
approximately $76.9 million in non-federal funding and approximately $15.9 million from 
other federal sources. The significant non-federal contribution strengthens the Project’s 
competitiveness and keeps the overall federal share below 50 percent. Results are 
anticipated in December 2026. 
Additionally, the City requested approximately $54.8 million through the Infrastructure 
for Rebuilding America program. The requested INFRA funding represents

approximately 37 percent of the total Project cost. Including other federal sources, total 
federal assistance would represent approximately 48 percent of the Project, with

Staff will continue evaluating and pursuing additional funding opportunities as they 
become available. The City’s funding strategy is intentionally diversified so that the 
Project is not dependent upon the outcome of any single grant program. 
Project Status 
 
The Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation Project continues to advance toward final 
design and construction readiness. The 65 percent PS&E package has been reviewed 
by Caltrans, and the 95 percent submittal is anticipated in September. Right-of-way 
activities, environmental permitting, and utility relocation design are progressing 
concurrently. Coordination with UPRR also continues, with the 100 percent track and 
shoofly design and updated structural plans recently resubmitted for review. 
While progress continues, several schedule risks remain, primarily associated with third-
party review timelines, utility relocations, environmental permitting, right-of-way 
requirements, coordination with the MDP Line 2 Project, and required agreements with 
Caltrans and UPRR. These items, along with the remaining funding gap, create 
uncertainty regarding the Project’s ability to achieve RTL by the current TCEP deadline. 
RCTC and the City will continue moving the Project forward as construction will proceed 
on the current schedule while aggressively pursuing the funding necessary to close the 
remaining gap. By continuing design, permitting, right-of-way, utility coordination, and 
agency agreements now, the City will position the Project to move into construction as 
soon as the necessary funding and approvals are secured.
