Current documented status

Council reset the project's funding strategy on August 4, 2026.

On August 4, 2026, the Beaumont City Council unanimously authorized staff to withdraw the Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation Project from the current Trade Corridor Enhancement Program (TCEP) funding cycle and immediately prepare a new application for the 2026 cycle.

Staff reported that environmental approvals, utility coordination, right-of-way acquisition, Union Pacific agreements, and Ready-to-List deadlines could not be completed in time to safely preserve the existing funding schedule. Rather than risk losing construction funding later, the City chose to voluntarily reset the application while continuing project development.

City staff reported that approximately $91.8 million in funding has already been assembled while an estimated $110 million funding gap remains before construction can proceed. Design, environmental work, grant applications, right-of-way acquisition, and coordination with RCTC and Union Pacific continue while the new funding strategy is pursued.

Why the project matters

Separating Pennsylvania Avenue from the railroad

The Existing Crossing

Pennsylvania Avenue currently crosses the Union Pacific Railroad at grade. Train movements can block the roadway, interrupt north-south traffic, and affect access between central Beaumont and the I-10 corridor.

Historical project records describe dozens of daily train movements and substantial cumulative gate-down time.

The Proposed Solution

The project lowers Pennsylvania Avenue beneath the railroad while maintaining rail operations on a new railroad bridge.

The project also includes roadway reconstruction, retaining walls, pedestrian improvements, utilities, freeway-ramp modifications, traffic signals, railroad work, and right-of-way acquisition.

Final Design Expands

Major State Funding

The project secured a major Trade Corridor Enhancement Program funding package.

Of the widely reported $50.4 million package, $49.4 million is programmed for Pennsylvania Avenue grade-separation construction and $1 million is associated with a separate Park & Ride EV-charging component.

5

RCTC Takes the Lead

In late 2025, Beaumont requested RCTC assistance because of critical funding timing requirements.

RCTC assumed lead responsibility for final design and right-of-way delivery and consolidated major project-delivery functions under a coordinated team.

Protecting the Construction Award

Environmental processing delayed the project, and the transition to RCTC added additional project-delivery time.

A one-year extension moved the construction allocation deadline toward the project's current 2027 delivery target.

Why did the estimate change? The public record documents substantial development of the project over time, including railroad coordination, Caltrans requirements, utility relocation, right-of-way, environmental processing, expanded design work, and updated project delivery. Beaumont Intelligence has not identified a single public cost-reconciliation document assigning the increase among those individual factors. The archive therefore presents the documented estimates without attributing specific dollar increases where the supporting evidence is incomplete.

A multi-agency project

Who is involved?

1

City of Beaumont

Project sponsor and original delivery agency. The City initiated the project, retained consultants, pursued funding, and continues to carry significant financial and project responsibilities.

2

RCTC

Riverside County Transportation Commission assumed lead responsibility for final design and right-of-way delivery to help advance the project within critical funding deadlines.

3

Caltrans & CTC

Caltrans requirements affect freeway interfaces, environmental review, engineering, and project approvals. The California Transportation Commission administers major state funding and related deadlines.

4

Union Pacific Railroad

The project crosses an active Union Pacific rail corridor, requiring railroad engineering, agreements, construction coordination, and continued rail operations.

Evidence matters

What the record establishes — and what it doesn't

Documented

  • Engineering and environmental work extends back to at least 2017–2018.
  • Approximately 35 percent design was completed in 2019.
  • The project later required expanded design, environmental, railroad, utility, and right-of-way work.
  • $49.4 million in TCEP funding is programmed for grade-separation construction.
  • Beaumont authorized up to $14 million for the RCTC-led PS&E and right-of-way effort.
  • RCTC assumed lead project-delivery responsibility during a period of significant funding schedule risk.

Still Being Reconciled

  • The complete allocation of the current approximately $140 million project estimate.
  • The final mix of local, regional, state, federal, and other construction funding.
  • The precise financial effect of changing engineering and agency requirements over time.
  • The final construction contract amount, which will not be known until procurement and award.
  • Any future changes to the construction schedule or funding plan.
