The at-grade crossing will be eliminated

Pennsylvania Avenue will be lowered beneath the Union Pacific Railroad rather than continuing to cross the tracks at grade.

The railroad remains operational

The project extends beyond the railroad crossing

Work also involves the I-10 ramps, traffic signals, utilities, sewer facilities, retaining structures, sidewalks, ADA improvements, and related roadway work.

✓

Beaumont, RCTC, Caltrans, the California Transportation Commission, Union Pacific Railroad, and other entities have roles in the project.

Why the story changed

Evidence of increasing project complexity

Railroad requirements

The project must maintain Union Pacific operations while replacing the existing crossing.

Environmental records identify an approximately 4,000-foot temporary railroad shoofly as part of construction.

Caltrans requirements

Project work extends into the I-10 interchange area and Caltrans right-of-way, requiring coordination with the state highway system.

Utility and sewer work

The project includes utility relocation and significant sewer work, including installation beneath I-10.

Right-of-way

Right-of-way became a distinct project-delivery phase assigned to RCTC within the state baseline structure.

Fragmented design work

RCTC records describe design development being performed through several separate, non-linked consultant contracts.

RCTC recommended consolidating design, right-of-way, and environmental support under a coordinated delivery team.

Funding deadlines

State funding carries formal programming and allocation requirements, making project schedule part of the financial story.

Delivery evidence

Why RCTC became central

The transition to RCTC should not be described simply as the City abandoning the project or another agency taking it away. The records show a coordinated change in implementing responsibility intended to move a complex multi-agency project through final design, right-of-way, funding requirements, and ultimately construction.

Beaumont

Initiated and advanced the project, managed earlier engineering and environmental work, and remains a project partner.

RCTC

Assumed the lead role for major remaining development phases and brought experience delivering complex railroad grade separations.

Caltrans

Has direct involvement because project improvements affect I-10 ramps and state right-of-way.

Union Pacific

Railroad operations and railroad engineering requirements materially affect how the project must be designed and constructed.

Verification boundary

What we should not claim

“The project cost $140 million.”

Not yet.

Approximately $140 million is the current published project estimate, not a final construction cost.

“The original estimate was wrong.”

Too simplistic.

Earlier estimates were prepared at earlier stages of project development. Later records reflect a more mature and complicated project.

“One agency caused the increase.”

Not established.

The record documents requirements involving multiple agencies and disciplines, but does not support assigning the entire increase to one agency.

“Construction starts in June 2027.”

Not established.

June 2027 is the currently announced Ready-to-List target, not a guaranteed construction start date.

“The project is fully funded.”

Not established by the records reviewed.

Multiple funding sources are identified, but the current approximately $140 million estimate requires continued funding reconciliation.

“The final price is known.”

No.

A competitive construction bid and award will provide substantially better evidence of the actual market construction cost.

Research assessment

What the evidence says today

Pennsylvania Avenue began as a locally managed effort to eliminate a dangerous railroad crossing.

As engineering advanced, the project became intertwined with railroad operations, the I-10 interchange, Caltrans facilities, utilities, sewer infrastructure, right-of-way, environmental requirements, and multiple funding programs.

The public record also shows periods of interrupted project development and a fragmented consultant structure.

By late 2025 and 2026, the delivery model itself changed. Beaumont, RCTC, Caltrans, and the California Transportation Commission formalized a structure in which RCTC would take a much larger role in moving the project toward construction.

The project's increasing cost should therefore be understood within that larger history. The records support a conclusion that the project became more technically, financially, and institutionally complex as it moved toward a construction-ready design.

They do not yet support a precise claim assigning every increase in estimated cost to a particular requirement, agency, design decision, or period of delay.

Evidence standard

How Beaumont Intelligence handles uncertainty

Verified

Supported directly by an official government record or primary source.

Supported interpretation

A conclusion drawn from multiple records, with the underlying evidence identified so readers can evaluate it themselves.

Estimate

A number published for planning, programming, budgeting, or project-development purposes that may change as the project advances.

Open question

A matter for which the available evidence does not yet support a definitive conclusion.

Intelligence assessment

Pennsylvania Avenue cannot be understood from one agency's records

The Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation began as a Beaumont capital project, but its public record now spans multiple institutions.

Beaumont Council records explain the early consultant structure and local decisions.

RCTC records explain the project-delivery transition.

California Transportation Commission records explain the state funding deadlines, implementing-agency change, baseline, and construction-allocation requirements.

Environmental and engineering documents explain why the physical project became increasingly complex.

Bringing those records together is what allows Pennsylvania Avenue to be understood as one continuous project history rather than a collection of disconnected government actions.

Research conclusion

A multi-agency project leaves a multi-agency record

No single document tells the Pennsylvania Avenue story.

Beaumont's records explain the project's origins, early engineering work, consultant decisions, and local approvals.

Environmental records define the physical project that ultimately had to be delivered.

RCTC records explain the transition toward a consolidated regional delivery structure.

California Transportation Commission records establish the state funding framework, deadlines, baseline, and implementing-agency change.

Read together, those records show a project that became more technically complicated, more expensive, and more institutionally complex as it moved from concept toward construction.
