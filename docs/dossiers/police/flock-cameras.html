Executive summary

What the verified record establishes

Fixed-camera ALPR dossier

Beaumont Police has used Flock Safety automated license plate readers since at least 2020. A later City staff report says the first arrangement allowed Flock equipment in public right-of-way within the Sundance homeowners association. Council expanded installation authority citywide in 2023 and approved a two-year, $225,900 subscription in 2024 for 36 additional cameras.

At the 2024 meeting, staff reported 11 cameras already operating. Adding all 36 would imply a 47-camera network, but the records reviewed do not confirm that every authorized camera was installed or remains active. Staff described the cameras as an investigative tool for identifying vehicles associated with crimes and stolen vehicles.

The live Flock transparency portal reports that Flock data is retained for 30 days. Beaumont Police Policy 465 says ALPR data downloaded to the server should be stored for a minimum of one year. The reviewed record does not reconcile those statements or establish the treatment of exports, evidence, or other copies.

Analysis

What the record means—and does not mean

Verified conflict

Two published retention descriptions need reconciliation

The Flock portal reports 30 days. Policy 465 says ALPR data downloaded to the server should be retained for at least one year. Both can be accurately reported, but neither explains whether there is a separate City copy, whether the policy is outdated, or how evidentiary exports are handled.

Inference

Forty-seven cameras is an implied—not confirmed—network size

Eleven reported existing cameras plus 36 approved additions equals 47. That arithmetic does not prove all approved units were installed, activated, or remain operational. A current inventory is needed.

Evidence gap

Policy requirements are not proof of compliance

The policy calls for individual logins, access records, regular audits, and reviewed sharing requests. Without logs, audit reports, sharing approvals, and complaint outcomes, the public cannot independently evaluate actual compliance.

Evidence gap

Anecdotes do not establish system-wide effectiveness

Police staff described examples involving burglaries, suspect vehicles, and stolen vehicles. Aggregate counts for alerts, false alerts, leads, recoveries, arrests, complaints, and cost per useful outcome have not been found in the reviewed record.

Frequently asked questions

Direct answers from the current record

What do the cameras collect?

Fixed cameras photograph passing vehicles and license plates. The resulting records can be searched by plate and reported vehicle characteristics and can generate alerts involving identified vehicles.

Are the cameras used to issue traffic tickets?

At the August 20, 2024 meeting, police staff said the cameras could not be used to cite drivers for conduct such as running stop signs because the image does not establish who was driving. That statement describes the represented practice at that meeting; it is not a binding restriction in the published ALPR policy.

Where are the cameras?

Staff said the additions were planned around entrances and exits, areas associated with crime, and other strategic locations. The reviewed record does not contain a complete, current, authoritative location and installation list.

Does an authorized search require reasonable suspicion or probable cause?

Policy 465 says reasonable suspicion or probable cause is not required before an authorized user searches ALPR data. The legal process required in a particular investigation depends on its facts and applicable law; the public record does not show how often warrants or subpoenas are used.

Can other agencies receive Beaumont data?

The policy allows sharing with law-enforcement and prosecutorial agencies for official purposes after a written request reviewed by the Administration Division Commander or designee. The reviewed source set does not contain the underlying recipient list, requests, approvals, or a complete description of direct network access.

Has the system been audited?

The policy says audits should occur regularly. The reviewed public record does not include published ALPR audit reports, access-log reviews, error-rate reporting, complaint findings, or disciplinary outcomes.

How effective is the system?

Officials have described successful examples, but the available public record does not provide enough aggregate data to calculate useful leads, arrests, recoveries, false alerts, or cost per successful outcome.
