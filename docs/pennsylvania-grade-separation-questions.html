Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation

Questions & Answers

The questions residents are most likely to ask

Project costs, grant amounts, contract authorizations, and actual expenditures are different things. Those distinctions are preserved throughout these answers.
Question 1

Why is the Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation needed?

Pennsylvania Avenue currently crosses the Union Pacific Railroad at street level.

When trains occupy the crossing, roadway traffic cannot pass. That affects local circulation, access to Interstate 10, emergency response, and travel reliability.

The grade-separation project removes that direct conflict by lowering Pennsylvania Avenue beneath the railroad.

Vehicles, pedestrians, and trains will ultimately move through the crossing on separate levels.

Question 2

Is Pennsylvania Avenue under construction now?

No—not in the sense of the main grade-separation construction contract.

The project is currently advancing through final design, right-of-way, utility coordination, railroad coordination, and other construction-readiness activities.

Beaumont Intelligence will not describe the project as “under construction” until the construction contract has been awarded and construction has been formally authorized.
Question 3

What does Ready-to-List mean?

Ready-to-List is a project-delivery milestone.

It generally means the plans, specifications, estimates, approvals, right-of-way, and other required project elements have advanced sufficiently to move toward construction advertising.

June 2027 was the target tied to the 2024 TCEP award. Following the August 4 withdrawal, a new target will be established through the 2026 TCEP application and continuing project-development work.

Ready-to-List does not mean construction has begun. Advertisement, bidding, funding confirmation, contract award, and notice to proceed generally come later.
Question 4

Why did the project fall behind the earlier schedule?

There was not one single cause.

One major documented cause was environmental processing. California Transportation Commission records attribute nearly 11 months of delay to changes in the environmental review approach involving Beaumont and Caltrans.

The project also still required substantial final design, right-of-way, utility, railroad, and other coordination work.

Transitioning project delivery to RCTC added additional onboarding time but was intended to improve the project's ability to meet later funding deadlines.

Question 5

Why did RCTC take over project delivery?

Beaumont requested RCTC assistance during a period of significant project-delivery and funding schedule pressure.

RCTC records specifically identify critical funding timing requirements as a reason for the transition.

RCTC also identified the need to consolidate design, right-of-way, environmental support, railroad coordination, and other project functions that had been distributed among several consultant contracts.

Question 6

Did RCTC replace all of the earlier engineering work?

The available record does not support that conclusion.

RCTC inherited a partially developed project, including years of engineering and environmental work.

HDR—the firm selected by RCTC to lead the consolidated PS&E effort—was already involved in Pennsylvania Avenue railroad and track-design work.

The evidence therefore supports describing the transition as a consolidation and continuation of project delivery, not as proof that the earlier project was discarded and redesigned completely from scratch.

Beaumont Intelligence has not identified evidence sufficient to quantify how much earlier design work, if any, ultimately had to be substantially revised or replaced.
Question 7

Why has the estimated project cost increased so much?

The public record shows that the project estimate changed substantially as Pennsylvania Avenue became more fully defined.

~$30M Early estimate Historical consultant-era estimate.
$45M 2023 estimate City planning record.
~$79M State baseline CTC/TCEP programming benchmark.
~$86M Late 2025 Discussed during the RCTC transition.
~$147.6M August 2026 staff estimate Supersedes the approximately $140 million June figure.

The record also documents significant project requirements involving Union Pacific, Caltrans, utilities, right-of-way, retaining structures, sewer relocation, temporary railroad operations, environmental work, and expanded project delivery.

What we cannot yet say: Beaumont Intelligence has not identified a single public reconciliation document assigning specific portions of the increase to railroad requirements, Caltrans requirements, utilities, inflation, right-of-way, design changes, contingencies, or other factors. Those factors may help explain the evolution of the project, but specific dollar attribution would require supporting records.
Question 8

Is the project really $140 million?

Approximately $140 million is the most recent public City estimate identified by Beaumont Intelligence.

It should still be described as an estimate, not the final construction price.

Final pricing will depend on completed design, procurement, bids, project-delivery costs, contingencies, and later project decisions.

Question 9

Didn't Beaumont receive $50.4 million for the project?

Yes, but the commonly reported number needs clarification.

$49.4 million

State records identify $49.4 million for the Pennsylvania Avenue grade-separation construction component.

$1 million

The remaining $1 million in the $50.4 million package is associated with a separate Beaumont Park & Ride EV-charging component.

So the amount associated with grade-separation construction itself is $49.4 million.

Question 10

Did Beaumont lose the $49.4 million state award?

No.

The funding faced a project-delivery deadline, and RCTC sought additional time because Pennsylvania Avenue could not meet the original schedule.

The original construction-allocation deadline was June 30, 2026.

The state process moved the project toward a June 30, 2027 allocation target.

The need to protect this funding was one of the major reasons RCTC became involved in project delivery.
Question 11

Does the $49.4 million pay for the entire project?

No.

The TCEP award is a major construction funding source, but it is not equal to the current estimated project cost.

RCTC public discussion specifically acknowledged that the state funding would not be enough to pay for the entire project.

Additional funding is therefore required.

Question 12

Who is responsible for the remaining funding gap?

The public RCTC record indicates that Beaumont remains responsible for addressing project costs not covered by available funding sources.

RCTC taking responsibility for project delivery did not mean RCTC agreed to finance every uncovered project cost.

Plain English: RCTC can manage delivery without assuming Beaumont's entire financial obligation.
Question 13

What is the $14 million Beaumont–RCTC agreement?

Beaumont authorized up to $14 million for the RCTC-led PS&E, right-of-way, project-management, and related delivery effort.

RCTC accounting records characterize the money as local-fund contribution revenue.

The $14 million should therefore not be described as a $14 million grant from RCTC to Beaumont.
Question 14

Is the $14 million included in the $140 million estimate?

Beaumont Intelligence has not identified a current public source-and-use schedule detailed enough to answer that question conclusively.

The current approximately $140 million figure should therefore not be independently reconstructed by simply adding grants, contracts, and earlier estimates together.

Those records may use different accounting assumptions, project phases, or estimate dates.

Question 15

Why does the CTC baseline say about $78.9 million while Beaumont says about $140 million?

The March 2026 California Transportation Commission baseline represents a state programming benchmark associated with the TCEP-funded project.

Beaumont's June 2026 announcement presents a later approximately $140 million overall project estimate.

The two records clearly do not present the project on the same financial basis.

Beaumont Intelligence has not identified a single official reconciliation explaining the approximately $61 million difference between those two figures. Until such a record is available, both figures are presented with their dates and context.
Question 16

Is HDR being paid to redo work Beaumont already paid for?

The available evidence does not justify that conclusion.

Earlier engineering work did occur, and the City later acknowledged that a 2022 design amendment did not contain all scope necessary to complete the project.

RCTC then inherited a project with multiple consultant contracts and substantial remaining work.

HDR was already participating in the project and was selected to lead the consolidated final-design effort.

Some earlier work may require revision as final railroad, Caltrans, utility, and other requirements are incorporated. But Beaumont Intelligence has not identified evidence supporting a claim that all or most earlier design work was simply discarded.
Question 17

Why couldn't Beaumont simply finish the project itself?

Beaumont had advanced the project for years and successfully positioned it for significant outside construction funding.

But by late 2025 the remaining work involved a compressed state funding schedule, Union Pacific coordination, Caltrans interfaces, right-of-way, utilities, final engineering, and multiple consultant disciplines.

RCTC brought institutional experience delivering complex regional transportation and railroad grade-separation projects.

The transition was intended to improve the project's ability to reach construction within the available funding timeline.

Question 18

Who actually controls the project now?

No single agency controls every aspect of Pennsylvania Avenue.

1

RCTC

Leads major final-design and right-of-way delivery and is the state implementing agency for the grade-separation component.

2

Beaumont

Remains the local project sponsor with continuing financial, policy, roadway, and local coordination responsibilities.

3

Union Pacific

Controls the operating railroad corridor and must approve work affecting rail infrastructure.

4

Caltrans

Controls the Interstate 10 facilities affected by the Pennsylvania Avenue design.

5

CTC

Controls major state programming, allocation, and TCEP funding actions.

Question 19

Will trains stop operating while the bridge is built?

The project is being designed around continued railroad operations.

Project records describe temporary railroad alignment work—a shoofly—that can move rail operations away from the permanent bridge construction area.

That allows construction to proceed while Union Pacific continues operating through the corridor.

Question 20

Why does the project include a future third railroad track?

Project records indicate that the permanent railroad structure is intended to accommodate future additional rail capacity.

Designing the bridge for a future third track prevents a later railroad-capacity improvement from necessarily requiring reconstruction of the grade-separation structure.

Accommodation for a future third track does not necessarily mean Pennsylvania Avenue construction itself will activate a third track.
Question 21

Why is Interstate 10 part of a railroad grade-separation project?

Because the railroad crossing is very close to the Interstate 10 interchange.

Pennsylvania Avenue must descend beneath the railroad and then transition back into the freeway-ramp area.

That creates direct design interaction with Caltrans facilities, freeway ramps, signals, traffic operations, utilities, and roadway geometry.

The project therefore cannot be designed as an isolated railroad underpass.

Question 22

When will construction actually begin?

A reliable construction-start date has not yet been established in the public record reviewed by Beaumont Intelligence.

The immediate target is June 2027 Ready-to-List and construction-allocation readiness.

After that, construction procurement, funding confirmation, bids, contract award, and notice to proceed still have to occur.

A projected procurement milestone should not be presented as a guaranteed construction-start date.
Question 23

When will the project be finished?

The earlier 2024 funding application projected construction completion in October 2027.

That schedule has been superseded by later project events.

Because the project has not yet reached construction award, Beaumont Intelligence does not currently treat October 2027 as a valid completion date.

A new reliable completion schedule will become clearer after final design, procurement, and construction award.
Intelligence assessment

The most important question is not a single number

Pennsylvania Avenue is unusually difficult to understand if the project is reduced to one cost figure, one grant announcement, or one projected construction date.

The project has evolved over nearly a decade as funding, design, environmental review, railroad requirements, Caltrans requirements, utilities, right-of-way, and delivery responsibilities changed.

The most important questions now are whether the remaining design and agency requirements can be completed, whether the available funding can be preserved, how the remaining construction cost will be funded, and what the final bid price will be when the project reaches the market.

Those answers will become more important—not less—as Pennsylvania Avenue approaches construction.
