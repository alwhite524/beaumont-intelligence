Evidence Standard

How Beaumont Intelligence uses these sources

  • Official project pages are used for current project status but are dated against older records when figures change.
  • Council agenda items establish what was presented and recommended; they are not automatically treated as proof of final action without additional confirmation.
  • Executed agreements, adopted actions, and formal agency records receive greater evidentiary weight.
  • Total project cost, construction investment, and construction-contract award are treated as different financial measures.
  • Historical concept drawings are identified as historical rather than represented as final as-built plans.
  • Where official sources conflict or evolve, the discrepancy is preserved rather than hidden.

Intelligence Assessment

The source register is part of the intelligence

The Potrero Interchange record spans multiple agencies, funding programs, engineering phases, Council decisions, and changing project estimates.

A useful civic-intelligence archive must therefore do more than summarize the project. It must preserve the documents that allow residents to verify how the project changed and why.

This page is intended to serve as that public source register and will continue to expand as new official records are created.

Consensus to receive and file

2025-03-04 · I.1, J.1, J.2, J.3

Potrero Phase II: funding changes and the recorded motion

Combined motion recorded; minutes omit a carried result

The minutes combine the fourth CIP amendment, RCTC agreement amendment, three-party construction-funding agreement and TUMF amendment. The CIP proposal adds $5 million General Fund, $600,000 WRCOG TUMF and $2,402,257 in Tri Pointe TUMF credits, with $13.5 million Pass Zone TUMF intended for construction.

Evidence limit / next records: The minutes name a mover and seconder but omit the result and vote tally for this combined motion. Obtain the adopted CIP and executed agreements before treating the entire proposal as verified final authority. TUMF credit is not unrestricted cash revenue.

2025-03-04 · I.1, J.1, J.3

Potrero agreement estimate and CIP totals are different measures

Staff-report accounting comparison

J.1 estimates the cooperative agreement at $75,819,000: $68,113,000 construction/construction management plus $7,706,000 PS&E/right-of-way. Its broader project table totals $79,306,257, pending the CIP amendment. I.1 shows a $79,252,046 component total and $71,904,000 current-budget caption; J.3 shows $78,952,046 with a $71,904,000 estimate caption.

Evidence limit / next records: The agreement estimate is not the whole CIP. Same-meeting captions and component totals are not internally uniform. Do not average them or pick a final project cost without the adopted CIP, RCTC agreement and reconciled ledger. These are historical estimates, not actual expenditure.

2025-03-04 · J.2, J.3

Potrero TUMF: allocation versus reimbursement

Terms proposed in the combined motion

J.3 proposes total Pass Zone TIP TUMF of $14.1 million. J.2 describes $13.5 million for construction, with WRCOG reimbursing RCTC directly after RCTC pays eligible costs. These figures have different scopes and are not two independent grants.

Evidence limit / next records: The combined minutes motion has no stated result. Executed agreements, eligible invoices and receipts remain to be checked. Project reimbursements do not establish restitution recovery or settlement payoff.

2025-04-15 · G.6

SB 125 project labels need reconciliation

Conflicting attribution across dated records

Active Grants Detail labels a $5 million SB 125/TIRCP award as Potrero, dated July 23, 2024. The October 15, 2024 CIP report attributes a $5 million SB 125 increase to Pennsylvania grade separation.

Evidence limit / next records: This is a project-label discrepancy, not evidence of two awards. Obtain the allocation resolution, award agreement and project ledger before reassigning funds or increasing confirmed funding.

2025-04-15 · J.5

Potrero landscaping creates an operating obligation

Approved unanimously

Council approved the Caltrans maintenance agreement. Staff describes three years of plant establishment plus 17 years of maintenance, for 20 years, with costs from Community Services operations.

Evidence limit / next records: This is not completion or a quantified maintenance appropriation. Caltrans takeover after 20 years requires coordination, not automatic termination. Obtain execution, mapped areas and costs.
