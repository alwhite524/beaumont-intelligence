Cost history

The estimated project cost changed substantially

1

Approximately $30 million

Historical consultant material associated with the early engineering effort identified an estimated project cost of approximately $30 million.

This was an early-stage estimate before many later design, railroad, utility, Caltrans, and right-of-way requirements were developed.

2

$45 million

Beaumont's 2023 Local Hazard Mitigation Plan identified the Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation at an estimated cost of approximately $45 million.

The document described the project as unfunded at that point.

3

Approximately $74.7 million

The project's 2024 Trade Corridor Enhancement Program application reflected a project cost of approximately $74.7 million.

The application included environmental, design, and a much larger construction component.

4

Approximately $78.9 million

The California Transportation Commission's March 2026 baseline agreement recorded a programmed project cost of approximately $78.891 million.

That baseline remains an important state-level benchmark for understanding the TCEP-funded project.

5

Approximately $86 million

During the late-2025 transition to RCTC project delivery, the project was publicly discussed as approximately $86 million.

RCTC discussion also identified a construction funding gap at that point.

6

Approximately $140 million

In June 2026, Beaumont publicly described Pennsylvania Avenue as an approximately $140 million project.

This is the most recent City estimate identified by Beaumont Intelligence.

Cost reconciliation

Why did the estimate rise?

The available public record documents a project that became more technically and institutionally complex as engineering advanced.

Railroad requirements

The project must maintain Union Pacific operations, construct a new railroad bridge, coordinate track work, support future railroad capacity, and accommodate temporary railroad operations during construction.

Caltrans requirements

Pennsylvania Avenue interfaces directly with Interstate 10 ramps, requiring Caltrans engineering, geometric review, traffic analysis, signal work, freeway-related design, and approvals.

Utilities

The project involves major utility coordination and relocation, including sewer work and specialized construction beneath the I-10 corridor.

Right-of-way

Property interests, temporary construction needs, utility rights, and project limits require right-of-way acquisition and coordination.

Environmental process

Environmental processing changed during project development and contributed approximately 11 months of documented schedule delay.

Project delivery

Final design and right-of-way delivery transitioned from a fragmented consultant structure to an RCTC-led consolidated delivery model.

What the record does not yet establish: Beaumont Intelligence has not identified a single public cost-reconciliation document assigning specific dollar amounts to each reason for the increase from earlier estimates to the current approximately $140 million figure. The factors above are documented project developments, but the archive does not assign financial responsibility to individual causes without supporting cost records.
Key remaining research question: A key remaining question is how much of the latest estimate reflects a formal RCTC re-estimation of the project as it moved toward construction procurement. Later estimates would have had the benefit of more-developed design and clearer Caltrans, railroad, utility, right-of-way, staging, and delivery requirements than earlier planning-level estimates. Beaumont Intelligence is continuing to look for the estimating or bid-readiness records that document that reconciliation.
State funding

Understanding the $50.4 million TCEP package

$49.4 million

California Transportation Commission records identify $49.4 million for the Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation construction component.

This is the principal state construction funding associated with the grade-separation project.

$1 million

The remaining $1 million in the widely reported $50.4 million TCEP package is associated with a separate Beaumont Park & Ride EV-charging component.

That component remains implemented by Beaumont rather than RCTC.

Why the distinction matters: The full $50.4 million should not be described as Pennsylvania Avenue grade-separation construction funding. The grade-separation component is $49.4 million.
Beaumont's delivery commitment

The $14 million Beaumont–RCTC agreement

Local funding contribution

Beaumont authorized a cooperative agreement with RCTC in an amount not to exceed $14 million.

RCTC records characterize those funds as local-fund contribution revenue supporting Pennsylvania Avenue project expenses.

What the agreement supports

The agreement supports final design, Plans, Specifications & Estimates, right-of-way, RCTC project management, and related project-delivery expenses.

What it does not represent

The $14 million is not a $14 million RCTC grant to Beaumont.

It represents Beaumont-supported funding for the RCTC-led project-delivery effort.

Construction comes later

RCTC records indicate that the current agreement does not fully cover the future construction and construction-management phase.

Additional agreements or amendments will therefore be required as the project moves into construction.

Funding risk

Protecting the $49.4 million construction award

Original deadline

The original deadline for allocating Pennsylvania Avenue's $49.4 million TCEP construction funding was June 30, 2026.

Delivery risk

By late 2025, environmental clearance, right-of-way, utilities, railroad coordination, and remaining design work created substantial schedule pressure.

RCTC transition

Beaumont requested RCTC assistance in part because of the aggressive schedule required to preserve the state construction funding.

Additional time

RCTC pursued an extension moving the construction-allocation deadline to June 30, 2027.

The funding deadline is therefore not merely an administrative date.

It has directly influenced how the project is being delivered and was a major factor in the transition from City-led final design to RCTC-led delivery.

How to read the numbers

Four different financial concepts

1

Project Estimate

An estimate of what the project is expected to cost at a particular point in development.

2

Funding Award

Money committed or programmed by a funding agency for an eligible project phase or component.

3

Contract Authorization

The maximum amount authorized for a specific consultant, agreement, or project-delivery function.

4

Actual Expenditure

Money actually spent. An authorized contract amount is not automatically proof that the entire amount was expended.

Beaumont Intelligence does not add these categories together unless the underlying records establish that doing so is appropriate.
Intelligence assessment

The financial story is still evolving

Pennsylvania Avenue's cost history reflects a project that became more fully defined as design, environmental review, railroad coordination, freeway interfaces, utilities, and right-of-way requirements advanced.

At the same time, the project secured substantial outside funding, most notably the $49.4 million TCEP construction award.

But securing a grant does not eliminate local financial exposure.

Beaumont has already authorized substantial local funding for project delivery, and future construction agreements and the remaining funding gap will be important decisions as Pennsylvania Avenue moves toward procurement.

This page will continue to distinguish between estimates, funding awards, authorized contracts, and actual expenditures as additional records become available.
