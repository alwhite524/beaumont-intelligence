Question 1

Has the downtown project been approved?

The long-range Elevate 2050 plan was adopted in 2024. Council authorized preliminary design in September 2025, then closed that CIP and redirected its budget in November; the GHD contract was terminated. In January 2026, Council directed short-term goals; on October 20 it directed Phase I design and an STB amendment. Construction funding and award return separately. The October outcome is transcript-based.

Question 2

Is there a final design?

October 20 advanced the discussed Phase I concept and design services, not a completed final design. Broader gateways, streetscapes, parking, bicycle facilities, public spaces, and roundabouts remain concepts subject to investigation, engineering, analysis, and later decisions.

Question 3

Will traffic lanes or parking change?

No final change is established here. Council discussion identified through traffic, freeway detours, truck routes, alternate routes, parking, and pedestrian safety as issues the authorized studies should evaluate.

Question 4

What will implementation cost?

October’s $68,850 combined STB design cost and $2–3 million Phase I construction estimate cover a smaller phase; no single figure represents the complete cost of Elevate 2050. In January 2026, staff cited roughly $5.5 million in prior Downtown spending and an early approximately $27 million estimate for public-right-of-way improvements, with no construction funding identified. The estimate excluded buildings, façades, and incentives and was expressly preliminary.

Question 5

Are the business awards grants?

The four recorded 2026 actions authorized forgivable loans totaling $150,000, including September 15’s Cluck and Moo award. Forgiveness depends on program and agreement conditions; authorization alone does not prove full disbursement, compliance, or final forgiveness.

Question 6

What should residents watch next?

Key records include the design contract and deliverables, traffic findings, project-level accounting, phasing and cost estimates, funding actions, environmental review, and any later construction contract.
