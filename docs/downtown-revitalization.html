Research status

Phase I streetscape design advances within the adopted vision.

Beaumont's adopted Elevate 2050 concept describes a more walkable, mixed-use downtown supported by streetscape improvements, public spaces, business activity, housing, mobility changes, and a stronger sense of place.

Council authorized a GHD preliminary-design agreement 3–2 in September 2025. In November, Council unanimously closed CIP R25-15 and redirected its budget toward Pennsylvania Avenue readiness; the GHD contract was then terminated. In January 2026, staff reported approximately $5.5 million in prior Downtown spending and Council directed a measured, smaller-step implementation approach. On October 20, Council directed the Phase I streetscape concept and STB amendment: $53,400 in added design services and $68,850 combined design cost after correction. Construction funding and award return separately; the October outcome remains transcript-based.

About this center

Summary & description

Summary

The Downtown Revitalization Intelligence Center follows Beaumont's adopted Elevate 2050 vision and the public decisions affecting its implementation. It brings together planning history, design concepts, mobility questions, business programs, funding actions, Council decisions, and primary evidence.

Description

The center distinguishes the adopted long-range plan from later engineering, contracts, appropriations, expenditures, and construction. It documents the 2025 preliminary-design authorization and reversal, the redirection of R25-15 funding, the terminated GHD agreement, Council’s January 2026 short-term direction, and October 20 Phase I design direction while the larger vision remains in place.

What the vision includes

More than streetscape improvements

The adopted concept connects physical design with economic activity and community use.

Walkable streets and public space

Sidewalks, crossings, landscaping, lighting, public art, wayfinding, gathering areas, and potential mobility changes.

Business and investment

Business attraction and expansion programs, dining and retail activity, façade improvements, and strategic public investment.

Housing and mixed use

Long-range opportunities for housing, services, shops, and daily activity within the downtown area.

Questions this center will track

From vision to accountable implementation

What is approved?

Distinguish the adopted concept from later studies, designs, contracts, property actions, and construction awards.

What will it cost?

Track planning, property, design, incentive, infrastructure, and construction amounts without combining unlike figures.

How will access change?

Follow traffic analysis, alternate routes, parking, pedestrian safety, truck routes, and the relationship to other transportation projects.
