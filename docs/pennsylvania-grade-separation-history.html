Chronology

From early planning to RCTC-led delivery

The project has evolved over nearly a decade. Dates below represent documented milestones, not assumptions about when work was completed.

2017

Environmental work begins

December 19, 2017

The City entered into an environmental-services agreement with Moffatt & Nichol for the Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation.

Later amendments show that the environmental effort ultimately included historic-property, archaeological, paleontological, and other work necessary for state and federal environmental review.

CIP 2017-012

The project became identified in City records as Capital Improvement Project No. 2017-012.

That project number continues to appear in later Council reports and project documents.

2018

Engineering design begins

June 19, 2018

Beaumont entered into an engineering agreement with IDC Consulting Engineers for the Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation.

City records later describe IDC's initial assignment as advancing the project to approximately 35 percent design.

Early project estimate

Historical consultant material associated with the project identified an estimated project cost of approximately $30 million.

That figure should be understood as an early estimate from a much earlier stage of project development.

2019

35 percent design completed

Initial design milestone

The City's July 2025 project-status report states that IDC completed the approximately 35 percent design milestone in 2019.

Project development then slowed while the City pursued additional funding needed to continue toward final design and construction.

2022

Final design effort resumes

1

IDC Amendment

In July 2022, Council considered a second amendment to IDC's professional-services agreement, adding approximately $1.295 million in authorized scope.

2

Scope later found incomplete

A later City report states that the 2022 design amendment did not include all scope necessary to complete the project.

That becomes important when additional design, utility, railroad, Caltrans, and right-of-way work appears in later agreements.

3

Environmental work continues

In November 2022, the City considered another amendment to Moffatt & Nichol's environmental services agreement to complete additional environmental-review work.

2023

Planning estimate rises

Approximately $45 million

Beaumont's 2023 Local Hazard Mitigation Plan identified the Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation at an estimated cost of approximately $45 million and described the project as unfunded.

The figure represents another dated point in the project's evolving cost history rather than a final construction estimate.

2024

Scope expands and major state funding is pursued

1

TCEP application

The project was presented to the California Transportation Commission through the Trade Corridor Enhancement Program with a reported project cost of approximately $74.7 million.

The application projected environmental completion in December 2024, final design in August 2025, construction start in November 2025, and completion in October 2027.

2

October 1, 2024

Council considered amended and restated design and environmental agreements intended to provide the scope necessary to complete project development.

IDC's amended agreement was authorized up to approximately $1.884 million.

3

Design remained incomplete

The October 2024 City record identified major components at varying stages of completion, including railroad bridge structural design, track design, Caltrans geometric approval drawings, and civil plans.

4

Expanded technical requirements

The consultant scope had grown to include additional sewer relocation, utility coordination, traffic signals, Caltrans studies, right-of-way support, retaining walls, railroad coordination, and specialized subconsultants.

2025

The project reaches a critical transition year

1

July 2025 — major state funding

The California Transportation Commission adopted a TCEP funding package widely reported as $50.4 million.

State records distinguish the package as $49.4 million for Pennsylvania Avenue grade-separation construction and $1 million for a separate Beaumont Park & Ride EV-charging component.

2

July 15 — Council status update

Beaumont City Council received a dedicated Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation project update.

The staff report documented the project's 2018 engineering start, 2019 35-percent design, subsequent funding pause, and the fact that the 2022 design amendment had not included all scope needed to complete the project.

3

Environmental process changes

State records later documented that the project initially proceeded under a CEQA statutory-exemption approach.

Caltrans Environmental then required preparation of an Initial Study / Mitigated Negative Declaration. After additional work and coordination, Caltrans and the City ultimately returned to the original statutory-exemption approach.

4

Nearly 11 months of environmental delay

The California Transportation Commission later attributed nearly 11 months of schedule delay to this environmental-review sequence.

5

November 18 — environmental approval

Beaumont approved the environmental documentation and the project.

This occurred substantially later than the December 2024 environmental-completion milestone identified in the earlier TCEP application.

6

November 18 — RCTC agreement

Council also approved a cooperative agreement with the Riverside County Transportation Commission for PS&E and right-of-way delivery, with City funding not to exceed $14 million.

The confirms unanimous approval and records a June 2026 Ready-to-List milestone, with a one-year extension available.

Late 2025

RCTC assumes the lead delivery role

Critical funding timing

RCTC records state that Beaumont requested RCTC assistance because of critical funding timing requirements associated with the state construction award.

At that point, right-of-way and utility work had not yet begun, environmental clearance was only then being completed, and railroad coordination remained a major long-lead activity.

Fragmented delivery structure

RCTC described the existing project as being developed through several separate, non-linked consultant contracts.

RCTC recommended consolidating design, environmental support, right-of-way, railroad coordination, and project-delivery functions under a coordinated team.

HDR selected for PS&E

RCTC selected HDR for the consolidated PS&E effort.

HDR was already involved with the project through railroad and track-design work, allowing RCTC to build on existing project knowledge rather than beginning from zero.

$6.462 million HDR authorization

RCTC approved approximately $5.874 million for HDR's PS&E agreement plus approximately $587,000 contingency, for a total authorization of about $6.462 million.

2026

State baseline and implementing-agency transfer

1

March 20 — CTC baseline

The California Transportation Commission approved the project's TCEP baseline agreement.

The baseline recorded a programmed project cost of approximately $78.891 million.

2

RCTC becomes implementing agency

The California Transportation Commission formally changed the implementing agency for the Pennsylvania Avenue grade-separation component from the City of Beaumont to RCTC.

State records cited RCTC's experience delivering complex multi-agency grade-separation projects.

3

Schedule extension requested

RCTC sought a 12-month extension of the deadline for allocating the $49.4 million construction award.

The original construction-allocation deadline was June 30, 2026.

4

Extension to June 30, 2027

California Transportation Commission staff recommended extending the construction-allocation deadline to June 30, 2027.

The environmental delay and transition to RCTC were identified as major reasons additional time was needed.

5

June 18 — new federal funding

Beaumont announced an additional $2 million in federal funding for the Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation.

6

Approximately $140 million

The same June 2026 City announcement described Pennsylvania as an approximately $140 million project.

That figure is substantially higher than the approximately $78.891 million state baseline adopted only months earlier.

Current path

Moving toward Ready-to-List

Final design

RCTC and HDR are advancing the remaining Plans, Specifications & Estimates work, agency coordination, railroad requirements, and related technical design.

Right-of-way and utilities

RCTC is responsible for advancing property, utility, and related right-of-way activities necessary for construction readiness.

Construction management preparation

RCTC has identified Pennsylvania Avenue construction management as a planned procurement, positioning the delivery team ahead of the eventual construction contract.

June 2027 target superseded

June 2027 was the Ready-to-List and allocation target associated with the withdrawn 2024 TCEP award. The 2026 application will request a later allocation year.

Ready-to-List does not mean construction is complete or even underway. It means the project is intended to be sufficiently advanced for construction procurement.

History perspective

What changed from the original schedule?

2024 projection

  • Environmental completion: December 2024
  • PS&E completion: August 2025
  • Construction start: November 2025
  • Construction completion: October 2027

Current documented path after August 4, 2026

  • Environmental approval: November 2025
  • RCTC delivery transition: late 2025 / early 2026
  • 2024 TCEP award: withdrawal authorized
  • 2026 TCEP cycle: reapplication authorized
  • Ready-to-List and allocation targets: to be reset
  • Final construction schedule: still developing
Important distinction: Earlier project schedules were forecasts based on information available at that time. Beaumont Intelligence preserves those forecasts because they help explain how the project evolved, but they are not presented as current milestones after later records supersede them.
What this chronology reveals

A project shaped by funding, scope, and agency requirements

The Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation did not move through a simple sequence from design to construction.

The record shows early engineering, a funding pause, an incomplete later design scope, expanding technical requirements, environmental-processing changes, major state funding, schedule pressure, and ultimately a change in the agency responsible for delivering final design and right-of-way.

Understanding those transitions is essential to understanding both the project's current cost and the responsibilities Beaumont still carries as the project moves toward construction.
