Agenda at a glance

Council will hold one public hearing, consider three action items and take up five consent items. The regular session also includes recognition of the Pass Patchers Quilters Guild and a Youth Council report.

  • Public hearing. Council will consider introducing an ordinance that shifts day-to-day treasury support to City staff and sets procedures for a Treasurer absence or vacancy.
  • Action items. Staff seeks direction to identify potential sites for a third fire station. The report also considers temporary coverage during Pennsylvania Avenue construction. Council will consider a three-year Cherry Festival agreement with an estimated $295,000 net General Fund impact for 2027 and review the 2027 Council calendar.
  • Consent calendar. The five items cover November 17 minutes, a $310,000 initial fireworks contract, facility painting closeout, a $360,000 weed abatement contract ceiling, and the process for board and committee appointments.

Public Hearing calendar

F.1 · City Treasurer duties ordinance

Hold a public hearing and consider introducing an amendment to Beaumont Municipal Code § 2.08.120. It removes the current list of locally prescribed operational duties, assigns administrative and technical support to the City Manager or designee, and provides for continuity during an absence or vacancy. Staff proposes a later second reading and anticipates no direct additional appropriation.

Action calendar

G.1 · Third fire station and temporary coverage

Staff asks Council to direct a search south of I-10 and east of Highway 79, then return with property options, preliminary acquisition costs and potential funding sources. The report evaluates six locations and estimates $13–15 million for construction and $3.5 million annually for staffing and operations. Temporary coverage during Pennsylvania Avenue grade separation is a separate question; options include adapting a building or leasing modular space.

Read packet source · Packet pp. 120–147

G.2 · Three-year Cherry Festival agreement

Consider a three-year MOU with Cherries of the Pass Foundation. The City would lead festival operations and retain final approval of event plans. For 2027, staff requests $550,000 in General Fund appropriations and $255,000 in projected revenue, for an estimated $295,000 net General Fund impact. Ticket projections assume 15,000 paid admissions at an average of $10.
