I.2 · Continued hearing on short-term rentals and fees

Consider the continued short-term rental hearing, ordinance, and fee resolution. The staff motion calls for introduction followed by a later second reading; the attached ordinance instead has a September 1 introduction recital and an October 20 adoption block. Proposed fees in the attached resolution include a $250 annual license, $100 inspection or reinspection, $100 late renewal processing, $2,000 appeal, and $10 replacement license. The staff report describes $500/$1,000/$2,000 daily fine escalation, while the attached ordinance still contains $1,000/$3,000/$5,000 fines. The intended final ordinance and reading sequence require clarification before either version is treated as settled.

Open interactive agenda and supporting documents (5)

Funding and fiscal context: Community Development would administer the program. Fees are intended to recover costs; fee and transient occupancy tax revenue are not yet estimated.

Question for consideration: Which fine schedule, notice periods, occupancy wording, and reading sequence will appear in the final ordinance? Align the staff report, ordinance, fee resolution, and presentation, then publish one consistent implementation schedule.

Attachment checks (3)

Fine schedule differs between report and ordinance

The staff report describes fines of up to $500, then $1,000 and $2,000 per day with two 15-calendar-day escalation periods. Exhibit A still states $1,000 for a first violation, $3,000 for a second within 12 months, and $5,000 for additional violations within 12 months. The briefing cannot present either schedule as the agreed final proposal.

Packet p. 494 · Packet p. 504

The presentation also mixes introduction and adoption language. These draft recitals do not establish that an ordinance has passed.

Packet p. 493 · Packet p. 498 · Packet p. 510 · Packet p. 514

Fees match the resolution; implementation materials need alignment

The fee resolution separates $100 late renewal processing from the $2,000 appeal fee and adds a $10 replacement license. The presentation retains older fee-table text alongside revisions. The report clarifies a total residence occupancy calculation, while Exhibit A retains the shorter two-persons-per-approved-bedroom wording. Final public guidance should use the final adopted text.

J.1 · Tilden-Coil services for the new police station

Consider the Tilden-Coil program-management and owner’s representative agreement. The staff motion proposes an overall $5,437,851 ceiling and Phase I services of $219,752 plus $21,975 contingency, totaling $241,727. The attached agreement instead limits compensation and initial Phase I authorization to $237,752, including $18,000 for BIM constructability and underground utility modeling. Later phases remain subject to further authorization. The motion, agreement, optional modeling scope, and contingency need to be reconciled before execution.

Open interactive agenda and supporting documents (5)

Funding and fiscal context: CIP PS-07 lists an estimated total project budget of $88,475,000 against $8,462,372 in current funding, leaving $80,012,628 unfunded. Current sources are police development impact fees, CFD funds, and General Fund money. Staff anticipates Phase I is funded; future phases currently are not.

Question for consideration: Is the $18,000 modeling option included in the intended award, and what service cap and contingency should the final motion and agreement use? Retain a clear requirement for later Council funding and phase authorization.

Phase I price and modeling option differ

The staff motion uses $219,752 in base services and $21,975 contingency. Agreement sections 4.01 and 4.01(a) use $237,752, including an $18,000 modeling option. The cost proposal lists the same $18,000 as enhanced Phase I services. Reconcile the requested award, contract cap, optional scope, and contingency.

Packet p. 536 · Packet p. 543 · Packet p. 544 · Packet p. 636
