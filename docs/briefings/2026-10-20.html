Public hearings

I.1 · Religious facility revenue bonds at 1780 Orchard Heights Avenue

Hold a TEFRA hearing and consider a resolution approving up to $10 million in tax-exempt religious facility revenue bonds for Calvary Chapel at 1780 Orchard Heights Avenue. The staff report describes this as the required local approval for the borrower’s financing; it states that the City would bear no responsibility or liability for the bonds and that this action does not approve development.

Open interactive agenda and supporting documents (3)

Funding and fiscal context: Borrower financing; the report identifies no City financial responsibility.

Question for consideration: Does the final resolution preserve the stated absence of City repayment obligations?

Read the staff report · Packet pages 487–488

I.2 · Continued hearing on short-term rentals and fees

Introduce the revised short-term rental ordinance and adopt a fee resolution. Proposed fees include a $250 annual license, $100 inspection or reinspection, $100 late renewal processing, $2,000 appeal, and $10 replacement license. The report describes daily fines of up to $500 for initial unlicensed operation, escalating to $1,000 and $2,000 after the specified 15-day periods. The total overnight occupancy limit would be twice the number of approved bedrooms. The ordinance would return for a second reading and take effect 30 days after final passage.

Open interactive agenda and supporting documents (5)

Funding and fiscal context: Community Development would administer the program. Fees are intended to recover costs; fee and transient occupancy tax revenue are not yet estimated.

Question for consideration: What staffing, inspection capacity, and public-notification schedule will support implementation, and how will the $2,000 appeal fee affect access to review?

Read the staff report · Packet pages 493–496

I.3 · Municipal Code provisions for City committees

Approve first readings for four committee ordinances, including creation of a Public Works Advisory Committee. Each committee would have seven voting members. Financial and Audit would include five residents and two Council members; Economic Development would include six residents or business owners and one Chamber member. Existing terms and natural attrition would guide the transition. Adoption would return at a later meeting.

Open interactive agenda and supporting documents (5)

Funding and fiscal context: Staff reports no fiscal impact.

Question for consideration: How will vacancies, current terms, and member qualifications be tracked during the transition?

Read the staff report · Packet pages 516–519

Action items

J.1 · Tilden-Coil services for the new police station

Award a Tilden-Coil program-management and owner’s representative agreement with a proposed overall ceiling of $5,437,851, while authorizing only Phase I now: $219,752 in services plus $21,975 contingency, totaling $241,727. Later phases would require funding and further Council authorization. The report estimates about two years to reach ready-to-list status. The cost proposal treats later-phase fees as preliminary and excludes certain construction general-condition expenses.

Open interactive agenda and supporting documents (5)

Funding and fiscal context: CIP PS-07 lists an estimated total project budget of $88,475,000 against $8,462,372 in current funding, leaving $80,012,628 unfunded. Current sources are police development impact fees, CFD funds, and General Fund money. Staff anticipates Phase I is funded; future phases currently are not.

Question for consideration: What funding milestones must be met before later phases are authorized, and how will excluded costs and future fee adjustments be controlled?

Read the staff report · Packet pages 536–541

J.2 · $80,000 sponsorship request for the 2027 IOA Championship

Consider up to $80,000 for the April 19–25, 2027 IOA Championship at Tukwet Canyon, compared with $40,000 for the 2026 tournament. Council would also be asked to make the policy’s public-benefit finding. Proposed benefits include 15 pro-am teams, City branding, and a $10,000 media plan. Staff says attendance, resident participation, and media projections have not been independently verified; direct economic impacts are not quantified.

Open interactive agenda and supporting documents (4)

Funding and fiscal context: The report identifies $222,313 available in the sponsorship account before this request and $142,313 remaining if the full $80,000 is approved. Costs of requested City promotional support have not been calculated.

Question for consideration: What measurable deliverables, participant-selection criteria, reporting requirements, and limits on additional City support should be included in the agreement?

Read the staff report · Packet pages 639–643

J.3 · Downtown Revitalization Phase I

Direct the next phase of downtown streetscape design for street trees, gateway features, and furnishings along Beaumont Avenue, Sixth Street, and Grace Avenue. The STB proposal adds $53,400 to an existing $15,800 purchase order, bringing it to $69,200. Staff estimates construction at $2–3 million, depending on amenities and methods; a construction funding plan would return later. The concept includes potential conversion of selected parking areas to planting areas and preservation of emergency access at Grace Avenue.

Open interactive agenda and supporting documents (4)

Funding and fiscal context: Professional services would use the Public Works operating budget. No construction CIP has been established; a future CIP and funding plan are still needed.

Question for consideration: Before final design, how many parking spaces would change, what would ongoing maintenance cost, and what construction funding and accessibility requirements must be resolved?

Read the staff report · Packet pages 656–662

J.4 · Banning agreement for Highland Springs Avenue improvements

Approve the Banning cooperative agreement for Highland Springs Avenue and Ramsey–Sixth Street improvements. Beaumont would serve as lead agency, initially fund applicable expenditures, and invoice Banning for actual costs attributable to its jurisdiction. Banning’s estimated share is $369,580, including 15% contingency.

Open interactive agenda and supporting documents (2)

Funding and fiscal context: The work is part of CIP R26-01, with a $4,384,761 project budget supported by CFD, Gas Tax, Measure A, and SB1 RMRA funds. Banning reimbursement is a share of this coordinated work, not a separate project to add to the full CIP total.

Question for consideration: How will reimbursement timing, cost overruns, and jurisdictional cost allocation be documented and enforced?

Read the staff report · Packet pages 688–691

J.5 · Tyler Technologies three-year enterprise renewal

Approve a three-year Tyler Technologies renewal for FY2026–27 through FY2028–29. The stated total is $978,037.43: $631,892.93 in IT services and $346,144.50 in estimated wastewater billing and transaction costs. The schedule includes 5% annual increases. Wastewater credit-card charges are usage-based estimates; staff also requests authority for the City Manager to approve usage overages.

Open interactive agenda and supporting documents (3)
