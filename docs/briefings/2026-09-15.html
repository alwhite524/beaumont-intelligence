Executive overview

Six action items, with major decisions on staffing, infrastructure, housing, and governance

Council is being asked to approve a $25,000 forgivable downtown-business loan, appoint a Finance Director and Deputy City Manager under employment agreements, award a $931,800 traffic-signal construction package, approve long-term affordability terms for a 66-unit housing project, and direct a restructuring of City committees and outside-agency assignments.

Action calendar · 6 itemsExpanded for meeting preparation
J.1 · Economic development

Cluck and Moo downtown business loan

Staff recommendationApprove a $25,000 forgivable loan for a new casual restaurant at 271 E. Sixth Street.

The loan would be forgiven after three consecutive years of operation. If the business misses the required milestones, the balance begins accruing interest at the City’s LAIF earnings rate. Staff says the application met the program threshold and would activate a space vacated when Cornerstone BBQ expanded.

What to watch

The packet says one designated staff member performed the formal scoring while other departments reviewed and concurred. Staff also expressly notes the inherent risk of restaurant incentives and the City’s more limited underwriting capacity compared with a commercial lender.

J.2 · Executive employment

Finance Director employment agreement

Staff recommendationAppoint Travis Hickey as Finance Director under an agreement beginning October 19, 2026.

The proposed annual base salary is $276,934. The package also includes a $22,980 annual cafeteria benefit, a $350 monthly vehicle allowance, specified leave, life and disability coverage, professional expenses, and CalPERS participation. Staff reports that more than 60 people applied and that Mr. Hickey currently serves as Menifee’s Finance Director.

Fiscal impact

The salary is reported as fully budgeted in General Fund personnel account 100-1225-6010.

J.3 · Executive employment

Deputy City Manager employment agreement

Staff recommendationAppoint Kari Mendoza as Deputy City Manager under an agreement beginning September 15, 2026.

The proposed annual base salary is $299,089.56. The package includes a $22,980 annual cafeteria benefit, a $350 monthly vehicle allowance, specified leave, life and disability coverage, professional expenses, and CalPERS participation. Ms. Mendoza has served as Interim Deputy City Manager during two periods and has more than 30 years of public-sector experience.

Fiscal impact

The salary is reported as fully budgeted in General Fund personnel account 100-1200-6010.

J.4 · Public Works · CIP R25-07

Oak Valley Parkway and Palm Avenue traffic signal

Staff recommendationAward Calpromax Engineering a $847,090 construction contract, plus an $84,710 contingency, for a total authorization of $931,800.

The work includes a new signal, left-turn pocket, median modifications, ADA curb ramps, pedestrian equipment, signs, and striping. Calpromax submitted the lowest of nine bids. Construction is expected to take about 14 months, including roughly nine months to procure signal poles.

Funding

CIP R25-07 would provide $835,800 and the Citywide Street Rehabilitation project would provide the remaining $96,000.

J.5 · Housing · Xenia Avenue and East Sixth Street

Density bonus agreement for 66 affordable and supportive housing units

Staff recommendationApprove the agreement with Xenia Avenue Housing Associates LP and authorize its execution.

The approved project has a 44-unit base density and a 50% density bonus for 66 units: 65 units restricted for low-income households for 55 years, including 16 supportive-housing units, plus one manager’s unit. The agreement records the affordability requirements and runs with the land.

Concessions and waivers
  • Reduce the required pedestrian walkway from eight feet to five feet while remaining ADA compliant.
  • Modify usable open-space and private-storage requirements.
  • Require affordability certificates within 30 days after occupancy.
Packet wording to clarify: the fiscal-impact section refers to a “relocation agreement,” although this item is a density bonus housing agreement.
J.6 · City governance

Committee restructuring and outside-agency assignments

Staff recommendationDirect staff to develop implementing bylaws, resolutions, and related documents for the proposed restructuring.

The proposal generally shifts boards and commissions toward seven resident voting members, a staff liaison, and—where appropriate—a nonvoting Council liaison. The Finance and Audit Committee would retain two Councilmembers as voting members.

Largest proposed change

Create a new seven-resident Public Works and Municipal Utilities Commission to review infrastructure, transportation, utility rates, traffic, parking, and the Capital Improvement Program before matters reach Council. Existing memberships would transition through vacancies and expiring terms.

Questions before the vote

Points worth clarifying Tuesday

J.1: What operating milestones, reporting, security, and recovery procedures protect the City if the restaurant closes before three years?

J.2-J.3: How do the complete compensation, severance, evaluation, and termination terms compare with peer cities and Beaumont’s other executive agreements?

J.4: How will the 14-month schedule affect the intersection, and what contingency remains if signal-pole procurement or construction costs rise?

J.5: Who will monitor the 55-year affordability covenant and supportive-housing commitments, and how are violations corrected?

J.6: Which current committees would be dissolved, merged, or changed, and how will responsibilities avoid overlap with the Planning Commission and Council?
