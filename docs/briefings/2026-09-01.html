Executive overview

Three public hearings and four action items

Council will consider the updated Sustainability Plan, introduce regulations for short-term rentals and e-conveyances, formalize Chamber sponsorships, allow one billboard face to become digital, adopt a citywide sponsorship policy, and direct a larger 2027 Beaumont Nights schedule.

I.1 · Public hearing · Long-range policy

2026 Beaumont Sustainability Plan

Staff recommendationAdopt the Sustainability Plan as an update to the 2015 Climate Action Plan and adopt the related General Plan EIR addendum resolution.

The five-to-ten-year strategy includes 37 community sustainability measures and 21 resilience measures. Its 2022 inventory attributes 63% of community greenhouse-gas emissions to transportation and 28% to building energy.

What to watch
  • The Planning Commission recommended adoption 4-1 after discussing reach codes, hazard maps, incentives, transit, recycled water, and staff capacity.
  • Measures are high-level actions requiring later feasibility review, engagement, and technical work.
  • Staff reports no added General Fund impact because implementation is assigned to existing budgets and workloads.

I.2 · First public hearing · Neighborhood regulation

Short-Term Rental Program and Ordinance

Staff recommendationIntroduce the ordinance and adopt the fee resolution.

The program would require an annual license for each rental, tax registration, a local responsible party able to respond within 60 minutes, two overnight occupants per approved bedroom, on-site parking, and quiet hours from 10:00 PM to 7:00 AM. Parties and commercial events would be prohibited.

Proposed fees
  • $250 annual license; $100 inspection or reinspection; $100 late renewal or appeal.
  • $500 for operating without a license and $250 for omitting the license number from a listing.
  • Revenue cannot yet be estimated; fees are intended to recover program costs.

I.3 · Public hearing · Public safety

E-Bikes and E-Conveyances Ordinance

Staff recommendationIntroduce Chapter 10.39 and adopt an administrative-citation schedule effective with the ordinance.

The proposal sets riding and equipment rules for bicycles, e-bikes, scooters, motorized skateboards, and similar devices. Sidewalk speed would be limited to 5 mph or lower when conditions require. City parks would be limited to designated riding areas, and uncertified electric off-highway motorcycles would be prohibited in public areas.

What to watch
  • Citations are proposed at $500, $1,000, and $2,000 for repeat violations within 12 months.
  • Parents may be responsible for violations by minors; limited safety impoundment is authorized.
  • Costs would be managed within the General Fund; citation revenue is unknown.

J.1-J.4 · Action calendar

Sponsorships, billboard conversion, and Beaumont Nights

Four separate decisionsThese are staff proposals, not final Council actions.
J.1 · Chamber MOU

Authorize $37,350 in sponsorships, including $30,000 for the State of the City, and find a public benefit. The adopted budget has $40,000 available.

J.2 · Lamar billboard

Convert the south-facing static display at 174 Beaumont Avenue to digital while retaining the north-facing static face. The Planning Commission recommended approval 4-0. Lamar would pay permit fees.

J.3 · Sponsorship Policy

Create an annual open application period, viewpoint-neutral review, public-benefit findings, full-cost accounting for in-kind aid, and Council approval of awards. The policy appropriates no money.

J.4 · Beaumont Nights

Direct a 15-date 2027 calendar and separately decide whether to authorize a licensed beer garden. Placer.ai estimated 33,000 area visits across 12 events in 2026, not ticketed attendance or unique people.
