# Beaumont Intelligence Briefing
## Beaumont City Council Meeting
**Date:** August 4, 2026
**Meeting Type:** Regular City Council Meeting

---

# Executive Summary

The August 4, 2026 meeting focused primarily on transportation infrastructure, economic development, and capital project planning.

The most significant action was Council's unanimous decision to voluntarily withdraw the Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation Project from the 2024 Trade Corridor Enhancement Program (TEP) funding cycle and immediately reapply during the 2026 funding cycle. City staff and the Riverside County Transportation Commission (RCTC) concluded that project risks and funding deadlines made withdrawal the most prudent course of action to preserve long-term funding opportunities.

Council also approved environmental actions for the Master Drainage Plan Line 2 project, approved a Downtown Business Attraction and Expansion Program grant for Tuscanos Pizza & Pasta, and continued public hearings for the Sustainability Plan and Transit Yard environmental review.

# Strategic Infrastructure

## Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation

This was the principal discussion of the meeting.

### Current Status

Staff reported:

- Approximately $91.8 million secured.
- Current project estimated funding gap approximately $110 million.
- Project remains the City's #1 strategic priority.

### Key Risks

RCTC identified several significant project risks:

- Utility relocation
- Union Pacific coordination
- Caltrans access issues
- Right-of-way acquisition
- Master Drainage Line 2 dependency
- Increasing construction costs
- June 2027 Ready-to-List deadline

Failure to meet the Ready-to-List deadline would result in loss of current TEP funding. 

### Council Action

Council unanimously authorized staff to:

- Withdraw from the 2024 TEP funding cycle.
- Reapply during the 2026 TEP funding cycle.

RCTC advised that this strategy provides:

- Additional project development time.
- Opportunity to pursue additional grants.
- Greater likelihood of successful project delivery.

The decision does **not** suspend project work. Staff and RCTC will continue advancing design, environmental coordination, right-of-way acquisition, and grant applications.
