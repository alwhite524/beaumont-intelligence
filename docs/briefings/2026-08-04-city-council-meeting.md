**Date:** August 4, 2026
**Meeting Type:** Regular City Council Meeting

# Executive Summary

The August 4, 2026 meeting focused primarily on transportation infrastructure, economic development, and capital project planning.

The most significant action was Council's 5–0 decision to voluntarily withdraw the Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation Project from the 2024 Trade Corridor Enhancement Program (TCEP) cycle and reapply during the 2026 TCEP cycle. City staff and the Riverside County Transportation Commission (RCTC) concluded that project risks and funding deadlines made withdrawal the most prudent course of action to preserve long-term funding opportunities.

Council also approved environmental actions for the Master Drainage Plan Line 2 project, approved a Downtown Business Attraction and Expansion Program grant for Tuscanos Pizza & Pasta, and continued public hearings for the Sustainability Plan and Transit Yard environmental review.

# Strategic Infrastructure

## Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation

This was the principal discussion of the meeting.

### Current Status

**Status:** Active – Funding Strategy Revised (August 4, 2026)

On August 4, 2026, the Beaumont City Council voted 5–0 to authorize the City Manager to withdraw the Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation Project from the 2024 Trade Corridor Enhancement Program (TCEP) cycle and reapply in the 2026 TCEP cycle.

The Council determined that preserving long-term funding eligibility outweighed remaining in the current cycle because of schedule and delivery risks.

Staff and RCTC reported:

- Environmental clearance is complete.
- Design is approximately 65% complete.
- The August 2026 project estimate is approximately $147.6 million.
- The previously reported $91.8 million funding total included the now-withdrawn $49.4 million TCEP award.
- Without that award, the City described an approximately $110 million funding need.
- Three federal grant applications and the planned 2026 TCEP application remain part of the funding strategy.

### Key Risks

RCTC identified several significant project risks:

- Utility relocation
- Union Pacific coordination
- Caltrans access issues
- Right-of-way acquisition
- Master Drainage Line 2 dependency
- Increasing construction costs
- Former June 2027 Ready-to-List and construction-allocation deadline tied to the 2024 TCEP award

Failure to meet that deadline would have resulted in deprogramming of the 2024 TCEP award. Following Council's withdrawal authorization, new Ready-to-List and allocation targets must be established through the 2026 application.

### Council Action

Council voted 5–0 to authorize the City Manager to:

- Withdraw from the 2024 TCEP funding cycle.
- Reapply during the 2026 TCEP funding cycle.

RCTC advised that this strategy provides:

- Additional project development time.
- Opportunity to pursue additional grants.
- Greater likelihood of successful project delivery.

The decision does **not** suspend project work. Staff and RCTC will continue advancing final design, right-of-way acquisition, utility coordination, Union Pacific and Caltrans coordination, drainage-project coordination, and grant applications.
