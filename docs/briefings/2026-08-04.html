J.2 · Transportation · Funding strategy

Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation

Council actionAuthorized the City Manager to withdraw the project from the 2024 TCEP cycle and reapply in the 2026 cycle.
ActionApproved
Vote5–0
Video1:28:35 · Vote 2:09:16

Outcome: Council voted 5–0 to withdraw the Pennsylvania Avenue Grade Separation from the 2024 TCEP cycle and reapply in 2026. Project development continues while the City seeks a later allocation year and additional funding.

Key points
  • The decision changes the funding strategy; it does not stop the project.
  • Design is approximately 65% complete.
  • The total project estimate is approximately $147.6 million.
  • Federal grant applications and other funding opportunities remain pending.
Fiscal impact

No construction appropriation was approved. The packet’s approximately $54.7 million gap included the withdrawn $49.4 million award; staff described an approximately $110 million funding need without that award.

What to watch
  • Why staff believes withdrawal is less risky than keeping the current award.
  • What amount and schedule are anticipated for the 2026 TCEP application.
  • How drainage, Union Pacific, Caltrans and utility dependencies affect the revised schedule.

J.3 · Economic development · Strategic plan

2026–2031 Economic Development Strategic Plan

Council actionAdopted the 2026–2031 Economic Development Strategic Plan as presented.
ActionApproved
Vote5–0
Video2:09:40 · Vote 2:30:50

Outcome: Council voted 5–0 to adopt Beaumont’s 2026–2031 Economic Development Strategic Plan. The plan sets five-year direction for business retention, targeted industries, retail corridors, strategic land and sites, quality of place, and City promotion.

Key points
  • Updates the City’s 2019 Economic Development Strategic Plan.
  • Uses six strategic initiatives and an implementation matrix.
  • Adoption sets policy direction but does not approve every future program or expenditure.
  • Future actions requiring funding or contracts must return to Council.
Fiscal impact

Adoption has no additional immediate fiscal impact. The completed planning contract was not to exceed $110,000; future implementation costs will return separately.

What to watch
  • Which initiatives Council wants staff to prioritize first.
  • How progress will be measured and reported.
  • Which recommendations are expected to return as near-term agenda items.

G.10 · Governance · Strategic priorities

2025–2027 Strategic Plan Priority Order

Council actionApproved the revised priority order used for staff work plans, budget development and progress reporting.
ActionApproved
Vote5–0
Video1:12:49 · Vote 1:13:46

Outcome: Council approved the revised order for the City’s 2025–2027 Strategic Plan priorities as part of the unanimously approved consent calendar. The update changes the order of major projects and consolidates Police Station design, funding and construction into one priority.

Key points
  • Pennsylvania remains the top priority.
  • The Police Department Station moves to second.
  • Stewart Park Phase 2 moves to third.
  • Approval does not appropriate money or award contracts.
Fiscal impact

No direct fiscal impact is created by changing the priority order. Project funding and contracts must return through separate agenda items.

What to watch
  • Whether Council changes the recommended order.
  • Whether any project is added, removed or consolidated.
  • Which priorities staff expects to bring back next.

J.1 · Downtown investment · Business incentive

Tuscano’s Pizza & Pasta Expansion

Council actionApproved a $50,000 forgivable loan and find that it serves a valid public purpose.
ActionApproved
Vote5–0
Video1:23:53 · Vote 1:27:26

Outcome: Council voted 5–0 to approve a $50,000 forgivable loan for Tuscano’s Pizza & Pasta through the Downtown Business Attraction and Expansion Program. The proposal supports an approximately 1,000-square-foot covered patio and continued downtown investment.

Key points
  • Proposed assistance is $50,000.
  • Full forgiveness requires five years of compliant operation.
  • Partial forgiveness may occur after three years.
  • The agreement includes repayment provisions if requirements are not met.
Fiscal impact

The proposed $50,000 loan would be paid from the Downtown Business Attraction and Expansion Program account.

What to watch
  • What expenses are eligible under the agreement.
  • How performance and forgiveness requirements will be documented.
  • How Council evaluates the public benefit and risk.
